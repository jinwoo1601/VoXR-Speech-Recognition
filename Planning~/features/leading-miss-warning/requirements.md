# feat-leading-miss-warning — requirements

Backlog **item 3** of the G1-locked `leading-miss-bar` design (design §9). Depends on items 1 (#125, `41ea036`) and 2 (#131, `747c8df`), both merged. Blocks item 4 (`chore/release-2.0.0`). Tracked at #130; #124 is closed.

## 1. What this feature is

**The author-facing half of the leading-required-miss bar.** Items 1 and 2 changed what the parser does and what the docs say about it. Neither touched what the parser tells an author *about their own grammar* at construction time. This feature closes that, and it has two deliverables that arrived from different places.

The behaviour everything below refers to — **the bar (DR-1)**:

> A candidate whose **first required element** matched nothing may compete and may consume its span, but may not fire.

Three properties do the work here, and each is load-bearing for a different requirement:

- **Positional, not arithmetic.** The bar reads no configuration and no threshold. A rule derived from it is therefore exact at *every* `minScore`, which is precisely what the existing score-based reachability test is not (§4.1, F2).
- **Uniform over element type (DR-2 *of this design*).** Unqualified "DR-2" below always means this one; the sibling *relation* is DR-2 of #74's design, named in full wherever it appears. The source is inconsistent here — `VoxrCommandParser.cs:1327` says "design DR-2" for the relation while `:1631` says "as DR-1 defines it" — so the qualification is load-bearing, not pedantry. "First required element" skips optional literals and optional slots and is indifferent to whether the element it lands on is a literal or a slot. Any construction-time restatement must use the same predicate the runtime latch uses, or it disagrees with the behaviour it claims to describe.
- **Winner-only.** The bar is applied to the round's winner. It is *not* established that a leading-missed candidate can never fire by any path; #126 asks whether a tied rival can. Nothing this feature writes may generalise past the winner (§7).

**Deliverable A — narrow the existing sibling over-warning.** The construction-time sibling scan tells an author *"so the wrong intent can fire"* about grammars where, since the bar, **nothing** can fire. The code defers exactly this, at `Runtime/Commands/VoxrCommandParser.cs:1641-1647`, in a comment that is the best existing statement of the problem. This is E-2's release blocker.

**Deliverable B — the new construction-time scan.** Canon §9's own scope: detect the hazardous grammar shape that produced #124's phantom — *"a bare pattern whose trailing tokens can begin a match on another intent's **non-initial** required literal."* It detects the shape; item 1 already fixed it.

**Ride-along — #128's three unpinned guarantees.** Test-only, same territory, ruled out of item 2 at its G0.

## 2. Why this is a feature and not a chore

Design §9 calls item 3 *"independently valuable and independently droppable."* **ERRATUM E-2 falsified the second half** at item 2's G0, when the human ruled that item 2 would document *nothing* about the over-warning. With item 2 silent, dropping item 3 before item 4 ships a false author-facing claim into a tagged release with nothing disclosing it — the class of claim #81 spent an entire feature reversing.

The false claim is precise, and so is its reach. A cross-intent sibling set whose discriminator is each pattern's first required element, at `D >= 3`, clears the `(D-1)/D` reachability test and warns — and yet when that word is dropped, both siblings are leading-missed, whichever wins the round is barred, and nothing fires. The remedy the message offers is inert: `disambiguateSiblingTies` cannot reach it either, which item 2 has already published at `Documentation~/command-recognition.md:490`.

**Nothing is mis-warned on today's demo grammar — by coincidence of arithmetic, not by construction.** Four of the five pinned cross-intent sets have leading discriminators and are all already withheld by the `(D-1)/D` test; the one that survives to warn, `mode_weapons@3`, discriminates on the last of three elements, which the bar never touches. An author who writes a three-element pattern with a leading discriminator gets the false claim today.

**Scope ruling (G0, 2026-08-28).** E-2's argument blocks the release on Deliverable A only — the false claim lives in the *existing* warning. Deliverable B is canon's §9 scope and remains what canon calls it. **Ruled: build both on this branch, A first**, so the release-critical half lands and is reviewed independently of B's difficulty, and the option to cut B at G2 is retained rather than assumed away. This resolves the scope gap between "why item 3 cannot be dropped" and "what item 3's canonical text says it builds." **That gap is a canon inconsistency, not a reading of it**, and recording the ruling here alone would have been the silent patch the workflow forbids: canon says item 3 is the new scan only, while E-2's non-droppability argument requires the narrowing. Ruled 2026-08-28 as **ERRATUM E-3** — erratum-plus-pointer in design §9 item 3, no design branch, no G1 re-lock, on the E-1/E-2 precedent. Full record: `Planning~/features/leading-miss-warning/architecture.md` §10.

## 3. Non-goals

- **No runtime behaviour change of any kind.** Editor-only. No score, threshold, flag, gate placement or match logic moves. If a warning appears to require a behaviour change to be truthful, that is a finding to file, not licence to edit.
- **#127 is ruled here, not built.** The eager-hold latency was filed to be ruled alongside this item. Its option 2 (move the gate below the hold returns) is a runtime timing change and needs a design amendment; its option 3 pairs a shortened wait with a diagnostic and is also runtime. **Ruled: neither is in scope.** The ruling recorded here is option 1 — accept permanently, and record the exception to the "a refusal must not lengthen a wait it is not responsible for" principle so the next reader of that comment does not treat it as absolute. See §6.
- **#126 is not fixed here.** Whether a leading-missed *rival* should be excluded from a disambiguation choice list is a scope question canon did not rule. F17 pins the winner-side guarantee and will produce the fixture #126 says does not exist; the rival-side ruling stays open.
- **Fork C is untouched** and has no user-facing existence.
- **No version number anywhere.** `CHANGELOG.md` stays under `[Unreleased]`; item 4 names the version (DR-10).
- **No new public API** — no field, constructor parameter, event, or changed signature.
- **The sibling scan is narrowed, not redesigned.** The relation stays exactly as DR-2 of #74 defines it, and the filter stays at the warning site rather than in `FindSiblingSets`, for the reason the existing comment gives: a later consumer that cares about the relation still sees it.

### 4.2 Deliverable B — the new construction-time scan

Canon specifies this scan in a **single clause** and nowhere elaborates: no firing condition, no message text, no exclusion list. The clause's "trailing tokens" are the tail the bare pattern *strands in an utterance*, not its own last elements — in the reported case `P = ["time","to","target"]` strands `track one two four four` and `P`'s own trailing token is `target`, not `track`. Construction time cannot see a stranded tail, so **every candidate rule is a proxy**, and the choice of proxy decides whether this scan is useful or noise.

## 5. Non-functional requirements

- **Editor-only, provably.** Both deliverables must be elided from player builds. The precedent is not uniform — three of the four pattern scans carry `[Conditional("UNITY_EDITOR")]` and `WarnOnExcessiveOptionalExpansion` carries nothing. (`RunValidationWarnings` is a fifth unguarded emitter in the same call block, but it validates slot values rather than scanning patterns; a reader counting calls at `:654-662` sees five, not four.) **Follow the guarded precedent**, and pin it by reflection (F11).
- **Zero player-build cost.** With the call elided, a player build must do no additional work at construction. The scan may allocate in the Editor; the sibling scan already does.
- **Construction-time cost is bounded, at whatever shape F7 rules.** If the adopted rule is pair-wise it is quadratic in pattern count like `WarnOnDroppableRequiredLiteral`, multiplied by optional expansion; if it is victim-side it is linear. The cost is not a reason to prefer either — it is stated so the adopted rule's cost is recorded rather than discovered. Whatever expansion cap it uses must be stated and its blind spot disclosed (F12).
- **No public API surface.** Integrator code compiles and links unchanged.
- **The warnings must be readable by a grammar author**, not only by a maintainer — the register of `BuildSiblingWarning` and `BuildDroppableLiteralWarning`, which name the fix rather than only the fault.

## 6. Dependencies, assumptions and consequences

**Depends on:** item 1 (merged, `41ea036`) for the bar itself; item 2 (merged, `747c8df`) for the doc surface F13-F16 edit. **Blocks:** item 4.

**Assumption, verified from source rather than inherited.** A barred round emits no result, and the recogniser builds a disambiguation prompt only for *emitted* results — `TryBuildAmbiguity` is called from its loop over the result buffer, and the tied-rival accessor is indexed by result index. A barred round returns before `_resultCount` advances and before the tied-sibling record is written, so no index exists for the recogniser to walk, and the rival entries it did write sit at or past `_resultCount` where the accessor's contract says nothing reads them. **This is what makes F1's withholding safe** — on a leading-discriminator set nothing fires by the winner path *or* the prompt path. It is asserted here because #126's stated preconditions imply the opposite; F19 pins it rather than leaving it to argument.

**#127 — ruled, not built.** Accept the lengthened wait permanently. The reasoning, recorded so the next reader of the sibling-tie comment does not read its principle as absolute: that comment's rule is *"a refusal must not lengthen a wait it is not responsible for"*, and it holds because a tied candidate may still fire after the wait, so lengthening buys nothing. A **barred** candidate produces no result at any wait length, so the only cost is how soon the speaker is told — which is a different trade, not a violation. The cost is real where `prefixHoldSeconds` is set (about 1.4 s on #124's reporter's configuration) and inert at the shipped default of `0`. #127's "also unpinned" note — that no test covers the `HoldExtendable -> None` transition — stays open with the issue.

**Consequence for item 4.** Deliverable A must land for the release. If Deliverable B is cut at G2, F13-F15 are cut with it, since they exist only to describe B.

## 7. Inherited errata and findings — carry, do not rediscover

- **E-1 — "an eager commit IS a fire" is false.** `Commit` routes through `FlushBuffer` -> `ProcessParsedResults` -> `ParseInternal`, where the bar refuses the winner anyway. DR-5's eager condition protects the **buffer**, not G-1. Do not write author-facing text implying the eager path is a second way to fire a barred candidate.
- **§6.2's "five or more" is false**, and the correction carries a condition: `AllowPartialMatch` routes an incomplete winner to `EnterPending`, so *"can never fire at any length"* holds **only where that flag is off**. F17 is written on the flag being **on**, which is the case the erratum leaves live.
- **The bar applies to the round winner only.** No text this feature writes may generalise to "a leading-missed candidate never fires" while #126 is open.
- **Issue text is evidence, not canon.** #129 asserted a barred round could score `1.00`; it cannot. That false claim propagated issue -> requirements -> architecture -> plan before an adversarial pass caught it. #126, #127 and #128 are read here on the same terms — §6's assumption paragraph exists because #126's preconditions did not survive the check.
- **A site list closed by grepping claim strings is not closed** (F21).
- **A leading optional cannot make two siblings disagree about leading-anchoredness** — it shifts the *authored* index only, and the required-element predicate skips optionals. The one mechanism that produces a genuine disagreement is `{?ship}` against `{ship}`: the same frame element, scoring alike, but only the required one credits `MatchedRequired`. This branch's first draft of F3 had the wrong mechanism, the wrong supporting test, and a justification ("the other side still fires") that is registration-order-dependent and therefore false as stated. Corrected before implementation; recorded so it is not re-derived.
- **Two consecutive fix rounds each introduced a new defect on item 2.** The PR-stage `review-pr` earned its keep; expect the same here.

## 9. Open questions

1. **Does the adopted scan need its own expansion cap, or does it inherit `MaxWarningExpansion`?** The sibling and droppable-literal scans share the constant (via `WarningForms`) but **not** a `KNOWN_LIMITATIONS` entry — `:665-708` is sibling-only end to end, and the droppable-literal scan states its own cap inside its own entry at `:584-585`. So the precedent is *one entry per scan*, and F12 should follow it rather than extend the sibling entry. What remains genuinely open is only whether the adopted rule reuses the constant or needs its own. Feeds F12.
2. **Should the narrowing withhold silently, or warn differently?** F1 withholds, matching the existing gate's two exclusions and the `:1244` precedent of refusing to warn where one side is refused outright. But a leading-discriminator sibling pair is still a hazard — the speaker's command goes silent instead of the wrong one firing, which `KNOWN_LIMITATIONS.md:655-658` already documents as a real cost (*"silence instead of a wrong action, with the speaker having to repeat themselves, and no help at all on an utterance where the discriminating word was heard"*). Whether that deserves its own warning is a scope question this feature does not need to answer to close E-2, and answering it would add a warning canon did not ask for.
3. **How far does F15 go?** The `:449` section is written as advice for a hazard nothing detects. Once something detects it, the section could be restructured around the warning — or left as advice with one sentence added. The smaller edit is likelier correct, but this is a judgement the architecture doc should make explicitly rather than by default.
