# Maintainer rulings on the ten open questions (2026-09-05)

Recorded in conversation, one decision at a time. Each entry: the question as put, the ruling verbatim, and what it changes in `03-critique-and-ranking.md`.

## 1. Human recording corpus and sealed holdout

**Put to the maintainer:** commit to a human recording campaign (10 speakers × 30 phrases × 3 conditions on Quest, or a smaller pilot first, or none), and accept sealing half of it as a holdout that is never used for tuning.

**Ruling: "yes and yes."** The campaign is committed at the proposed scale; sealing half is accepted. First step per the critique: a pilot of two speakers × the existing 16 phrases, to see whether the pass rate moves, while the recorder scene is built.

**Changes:** R5 is GO and starts now (long pole). Items from rank 6 downward become rankable once it lands. The recorder scene + manifest is delegable engineering; the sessions are human-only.

## 2. `scoring.md`: contract or description

**Put to the maintainer:** the guide publishes five 699-row measurements and two enumerations declared exhaustive. Are they a contract (moving them is a breaking change), a description (re-derived and re-published whenever behaviour changes), or a split (lists are API, numbers are description)?

**Ruling: "description."** The numbers are regenerated by the pin tooling (`DocCheck` against the real parser; the joined audio rig for acoustic figures) and never edited by hand; the enumerations are updated in the same PR as the behaviour, with a changelog entry.

**Changes:** R14, R15, R13, R12, R27 are no longer gated on a major version. R17 and R22 may extend the eager and `OnUnrecognisedSpeech` enumerations. Standing rule for every such PR: re-pin, never edit.

## 3. Is the leading-required-miss bar absolute?

**Put to the maintainer:** may positive evidence that the first required element was spoken (an N-best alternative, a phonetic near-match, a strong-evidence "did you mean?") change a barred round's outcome, or is refuse-to-fire absolute?

**Ruling: absolute.** The accepted transcript must contain the first required element. No phonetic, fuzzy or evidence-based lift exists at the anchor. N-best parsing may change *which* transcript is accepted (each alternative is parsed and barred on its own terms); that is selection, not relaxation. Any softer form (ask-on-evidence, fire-on-evidence) requires reopening the leading-miss-bar design doc on a design branch with human-corpus evidence of the bar's cost, per the workflow. Maintainer's stated reason: "I don't want commands firing that were not of user's intention."

**Clarified in passing:** optional elements are transparent to the bar in every form (the anchor is the first *required* element; `VoxrCommandParser.cs:3561`, `:3612`). Making a verb optional moves the anchor onto the next required element — an authoring hazard independent of this ruling; candidate for a construction-time note later.

**Changes:** R12 (N-best) keeps its full case. R25 (phonetic matching) is restricted to non-anchor positions and drops in value; stays deferred behind R14. The ask route is closed until a design branch reopens it.

## 4. N-best first, or the confidence display first?

**Put to the maintainer:** N-best mode deletes per-word `conf` (every word arrives as −1, silently disabling the `minConfidence` gate and eager condition 7), and the confidence display consumes exactly that number. Which stream goes first?

**Ruling: N-best first** (maintainer: "go for your recommendation"). Two conditions attach:
1. Run the free NCE/ECE information measurement of grammar-mode `conf` on the existing session logs now, regardless of order.
2. The N-best replacement signal must be designed once, with a per-word component (e.g. which words differ across alternatives) specified as the confidence display's input, alongside the per-hypothesis margin and parse-agreement that replace the two gates.

**Changes:** R12 gains a hard requirement — it cannot ship without a replacement for both gates and the display's per-word input in the same change. R31 (display) waits for R12 and is built on the new signal, not on today's `conf`. R26 (calibrated reliability) stays deferred behind the human corpus. R23 (token-aligned confidence) still lands first as the carrier for whatever per-word number replaces `conf`.

## 5. Voice-activity detector and its dependency

**Put to the maintainer:** which dependency class is acceptable for a VAD gate — Silero + ONNX Runtime (MIT, ~2 MB model + ~15 MB runtime), TEN VAD (532 KB, prebuilt-only, Apache-2.0 *with additional conditions*), the WebRTC audio-processing module (BSD-3, source-only, includes AGC2 with its own VAD), or none?

**Licence fact established during the decision (fetched `TEN-framework/ten-vad` LICENSE and README):** the additional conditions are a non-compete with Agora's offerings and a clause limiting deployment to "your Application(s) solely for your benefit and the benefit of your direct End Users"; distributed as prebuilt binaries only. Ambiguous at best for a redistributed SDK whose players are not VoXR's direct end users. **TEN VAD is rejected on licence.**

**Latency question answered:** onset costs nothing when the gate opens with 200–300 ms of pre-roll from the ring buffer (decoder catches up in tens of ms; the event is timed by end of speech); offset is a net *cut* on the pending/ambiguous/incomplete paths when the VAD owns the end-of-utterance decision (200–300 ms hangover replaces 500–1000 ms endpointer + 500–2000 ms buffer window). Design trap: withholding audio while leaving Kaldi's endpointer in charge stalls it — feed zeros or take over the endpoint. Eager commit is unaffected. Compute is <1 % of a core either way.

**Ruling: WebRTC audio-processing module first** (maintainer: "go for your recommendation"). It arrives with the AGC2 replacement item 7 needs, adds no inference runtime, and is licence-clean. Silero + ONNX Runtime is held as the upgrade path if (a) the free field-log probe shows non-speech triggers matter and (b) the WebRTC VAD proves too weak on the 30-cough offline set. The two free probes run before any code.

**Changes:** R15 (AGC) and R16 (VAD) merge into one dependency decision: bring in WebRTC APM, use AGC2 as the gain control and its VAD as the gate and endpoint signal. R16's kill criterion stands (first-word retention must not fall at any false-trigger reduction). An ONNX Runtime dependency does not enter the package this cycle unless the upgrade path is triggered.

## 6. APK budget

**Put to the maintainer:** a ceiling for the package's contribution to a game's APK (today ~49 MB: 40 MB model zip + 9 MB `libvosk.so`), and whether download-on-demand is acceptable.

**Ruling:** ceiling raised to **70 MB** for what ships inside the APK. **Models are downloaded on demand at a language-selection screen in shipped builds** — this is the maintainer's stated intention, so the model's 40 MB moves out of the APK budget entirely once model management exists. The `0.22-lgraph` screen is **agreed** as a measurement.

**Changes:** the 70 MB ceiling admits the Silero + ONNX Runtime upgrade path (~+17 MB) and the sherpa-onnx Zipformer screen (~+12 MB) if either is triggered; the +88 MB `0.22-lgraph` model can never ship inside the APK but could ship as a downloadable model. R41's `0.22-lgraph` screen is GO after R1 (join the rigs), as a measurement only (C12: no TTS-only comparison ranks decoders). **New item, not previously in the register:** model download-on-demand with a language-selection flow (from the old v6 roadmap: CDN fetch, progress, caching, integrity check) becomes a committed feature; it interacts with R9 (the cache version key) — the key must cover downloaded models as well as StreamingAssets zips.

## 7. Wrong-mode explainability as a product goal

**Put to the maintainer:** should the package be able to tell the game which *inactive* set an utterance belonged to ("that's a navigation command; you're in weapons mode"), at the cost of a wider decoder grammar?

**Ruling: yes, as a measured experiment, not a commitment** (maintainer: "go with your recommendation"). The union grammar with a shadow parse and an `OnCommandOutOfContext` event (R22) is tried once the rigs are joined (R1). Kill criterion: any measurable loss on in-set commands and it stays a documented workaround. The parallel free-vocabulary decode (R29) remains BLOCKED.

**Changes:** R22 moves from deferred to "screen after R1", depending on R13 (weighted emission) whose mechanism it rides on. If R22 survives, the set-switch audio gap disappears by construction and R19 (keep capture running) narrows to dynamic-slot and explicit rebuilds. If R22 is killed, R19 is the audio-gap fix.

## 8. Speculative execution with retraction

**Put to the maintainer:** execute reversible commands on the partial hypothesis and retract if the final contradicts it (~200 ms gain, occasional visible undo), versus acknowledge-at-partial and execute-at-final only; and which real-game commands are reversible.

**Ruling: no for this cycle.** Acknowledge-first (R30) is the only early path. R37 is not built; no per-command "reversible" flag, no retraction event. Revisit only with a measured partial-vs-final contradiction rate from R17's instrumentation, and only as a design branch, since it stands in direct tension with the phantom-command ruling (a retraction nobody notices is a phantom with extra steps; users detect ~2 % of insertions).

**Changes:** R37 moves from deferred to closed-for-cycle. R17's contradiction measurement is still taken (it is R17's own kill criterion) and becomes the evidence any future reopening must cite. The reversible-command list is not requested.

## 9. Deixis: target registry, and scope this cycle

**Put to the maintainer:** does the game already publish targetable entities with categories and salience (sets the cost of deixis), and which phase — ellipsis, pronouns/demonstratives, gaze fusion — does the package commit to this cycle?

**Ruling:** (1) the registry **would need building**. (2) **Ellipsis only** this cycle. The maintainer pointed to the VR FTL-Like 3 `brain-dump.md` entry of 2026-08-19 ("Main target / unspoken target for aggressive orders"), which redefines the feature from the game side: the track number becomes optional in aggressive orders when one obvious target exists — designated by the player ("track 1721 is the main target"), implicitly highlighted through interception, or the only hostile on the map.

**Definition adopted:** ellipsis is **game-state resolution of an omitted required slot**, not "reuse the last-mentioned target". Rationale: never guesses from dialogue history (a stale last target cannot aim a missile at the wrong ship), and it places the resolution where the knowledge is — the game.

**Split of work:**
- *Package, this cycle:* a required slot may be marked resolvable; when the transcript leaves it empty, the parser asks a game-supplied resolver (`Func<string>`-shaped, per slot) before ruling the command incomplete. Returns a value or nothing; nothing → today's behaviour (pending or rejected). The resolver reports which rule it used so readback can name the target. Testable entirely through `InjectText`. No grammar change, no timing plumbing — R23 and the timing rebase are **not** prerequisites for this phase.
- *Game, later:* main-target designation (including the designation order), interception highlight, only-hostile rule. Aggressive orders only.
- *Deferred to a design branch after `human-core`:* pronouns and demonstratives (`{target*}` slot, ~10 grammar words), gaze/pointing fusion on per-word timings (needs R23 + rebase).

**Changes:** R36 splits. R36a (resolver hook) is S-effort, unblocked, and rides on the existing dynamic-slot/pending machinery. R36b/c stay deferred. The brain-dump entry should move into the workflow (G0) when the package hook lands, per that file's own rule.

## 10. May the front-end fixes shift every published score?

**Put to the maintainer:** items 7 (AGC unify + gate) and 8 (decimation filter) change what the decoder hears, so they move the acoustic harness baselines, the WAV-replay PlayMode expectations, quoted confidence values, and thresholds tuned against the old front end — none of which the parser pin tooling can regenerate. Proceed ahead of the human corpus, freeze the front end, or gate on the corpus?

**Ruling: yes, re-baseline** (maintainer: "go with your recommendation"). Conditions:
- R14 and R15 land as **one evaluated unit** through the joined rig (R1), because the filter removes +2.51 dB of DC gain the AGC currently absorbs; separate evaluation misattributes.
- Acoustic baselines (`expectations.json`, WAV-replay expectations) are **regenerated by running the harness, never hand-edited**. Every fixture whose outcome changes is listed in the PR with old and new transcript; any pass→fail flip needs a named explanation before merge.
- Documented confidence examples are re-read from the session log. Sample-grammar aliases that exist to dodge substitutions stay until the human corpus says otherwise.
- The decision-1 pilot (two speakers × 16 phrases), if it exists by then, is the human check; the full campaign re-ranks later like everything else.

**Changes:** R14 + R15 are unblocked ahead of R5; their acceptance gate is R1 plus the pilot, not the full corpus. Decision 2's "re-pin, never edit" rule is extended from the parser pins to the acoustic baselines.
