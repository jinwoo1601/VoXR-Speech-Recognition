# I2 — Redesigning the command-matching layer

Ideation report, 2026-09-05. Scope: stage §2.6 of `00-brief.md` (the parser) and the two stages it is coupled to — §2.4 (grammar generation, which the parser owns) and §2.5 (the utterance buffer that feeds it). Grounded in `00-brief.md`, `02-verified-facts.md`, `01-code-audit.md`, R1/R2/R3/R5/R6, `Documentation~/scoring.md`, `Documentation~/command-recognition.md`, and the bodies of `Runtime/Commands/VoxrCommandParser.cs`.

**R4 (`research/R4-frontend-robustness-personalization.md`) was not on disk at the time of writing and is not reflected here.** Anything it says about front-end robustness, speaker adaptation (the shipped model *does* use online i-vectors — `02-verified-facts.md`), or personalization may add proposals this report does not contain.

Every performance claim is tagged **measured** (with its source and its corpus) or **unmeasured**. No claim about VoXR's own accuracy or latency under any proposal here is measured, because no instrument in the project can currently measure one (R6 exec summary; see M0 in the roadmap).

---

## Thesis

The matching layer today is a string matcher that spends most of its ~60 rules reconstructing, from a single argmax transcript, evidence the decoder had and threw away: which words competed, which were likely deleted, how torn the posterior was, and where in time each word sat. `VoxrCommandParser` receives `string[] tokens` plus a `Dictionary<string,float>` keyed by *word text* (`VoxrCommandParser.cs:3821-3830`) and consults nothing else — timings are parsed (`Runtime/VoxrJsonParser.cs:52`) and discarded, alternatives are never requested, and the grammar the parser itself generates (`:3076`) deliberately deduplicates through a `HashSet<string>` (`:3079`), discarding the one weighting lever VOSK offers. What it should become is a **decision layer over a distribution**: it consumes N-best hypotheses and per-token posteriors instead of one string, pushes structure the decoder can enforce back into the decoder (phrase priors, an `[unk]` prior, a vocabulary gate, phone-level knowledge), keeps the arithmetic it needs and learns the constants it currently hand-sets, and emits **one calibrated reliability number** in place of two uncalibrated thresholds over incomparable units. The rules that encode *safety invariants* rather than arithmetic — the leading-required-miss bar (E6–E8), the phantom-command prohibition it implements, completeness (R4), and the determinism of selection (A4) — are constraints on that layer, not candidates for subsumption by a probability model.

## Proposals

### M1 — Match against N-best, score (hypothesis × parse) jointly, and replace `minConfidence` with a hypothesis-level margin

**Problem.** The three named substitution failures are all cases where the correct string almost certainly exists below rank 1: `switch to weapons` → `switch two weapons` (`KNOWN_LIMITATIONS.md:19`), `all modes` → `fall modes` (`:54`), `cease fire` → `safe five` (`:71`). The parser cannot see rank 2 because the bridge never calls `vosk_recognizer_set_max_alternatives`, which the shipped arm64 `libvosk.so` **does** export (`02-verified-facts.md`; R1 finding 1, verified by `nm -D` locally). Everything downstream — score, coverage, the bar, sibling ties — is conditioned on an argmax nobody checked.

**Mechanism.** Three layers.
1. *Bridge*: `vosk_recognizer_set_max_alternatives(g_recognizer, N)` after each recognizer construction (`NativeBridge~/src/vosk_bridge.cpp:225`, `:363`), N=3 initially. `Recognizer::NbestResult` then emits `{"alternatives":[{text, confidence, result:[{word,start,end}]}]}`.
2. *JSON*: `VoxrJsonParser` learns the `alternatives` array; `VoxrResult` gains an alternatives collection (additive to the readonly struct — audit §6.5 notes adding a field is source-compatible).
3. *Parser*: `ParseInternal` (`:2455`) runs unchanged **per alternative**. A new selector above it ranks alternatives by a joint score
   `J(h) = log softmax_τ(likelihood(h)) + λ · log(bestParseScore(h))`,
   where `likelihood` is the alternative's `confidence` field — an unnormalised graph+acoustic log-likelihood (R2 finding 3; vosk-api issue #604 reports observed values ~50 to >500), so the softmax is over the N-best list only and is *not* a posterior over all hypotheses. λ and τ are two constants, fitted once (M8).

The load-bearing trap: **in N-best mode VOSK stops emitting per-word `conf` entirely** (`NbestResult` writes only `word`/`start`/`end` — R2 finding 3). `VoxrCommand.Confidence` would silently go to the `-1` "no data" path and `minConfidence` would be bypassed on every utterance (S14, `scoring.md:276`). The replacement signals, all computable from the same payload and strictly more informative than a min-over-span of a grammar-conditioned MBR posterior:
- **Posterior margin** — softmax gap between the best-parsing alternative and the best-parsing alternative that yields a *different* intent. This is the quantity `minConfidence` was always trying to be.
- **Parse agreement** — the fraction of the N alternatives that parse to the same intent with the same slot values. Disagreement across alternatives is a sibling tie *discovered acoustically*, which the T-rules can consume directly.
- **Rank of the winner** — firing on alternative 3 is a different event from firing on alternative 1 and belongs in the session log.

**Grounding.** R2 finding 3 and R2 recommendation 1 (symbol verified in the shipped binary; `02-verified-facts.md` confirms `set_max_alternatives` is exported and `set_endpointer_*` are not). R1 finding 1 (independent verification). Published effect for a *symbolic* downstream consumer: SLURP intent F1 **0.73 (1-best) → 0.77 (word confusion network)**, 5.5% relative (Villatoro-Tello et al. 2023, via R2 finding 11) — **measured, different system, neural NLU**. Prerequisite: `--lattice-beam=2.0` in the shipped `conf/model.conf` is one third of vosk's own default 6.0 (`02-verified-facts.md`, verbatim file), so N-best at the shipped config may return near-duplicates.

**Code impact.** Stages 3 → 6. `NativeBridge~/src/vosk_bridge.cpp` (one call, plus the vendored-header question — `NativeBridge~/include/vosk_api.h:29` already declares `set_max_alternatives`, so no header change, unlike `set_grm`). `Runtime/VoxrJsonParser.cs:52` (new array shape). `Runtime/VoxrResult.cs` (additive). `Runtime/Commands/VoxrCommandRecogniser.cs:566` `HandleResult` and `:719` (the `ParseInternal` call becomes a loop). New internal interface: the first one in `Runtime/` (audit §4.1: there is no C# interface anywhere) — `IHypothesisSource`, so the alternatives loop can be tested with injected lists through the existing `InjectText` seam (`VoxrCommandRecogniser.cs:299`). Parser buffers are pooled and re-entrancy is "pre-existing and unsupported" (audit §4.3), so the N alternatives must be parsed and *snapshotted* one at a time, not interleaved.

**Rules subsumed / kept / broken.**
- *Kept unchanged*: S1–S13, A1–A8, E1–E13, T1–T15, G1–G11 — every one of them runs per alternative exactly as today. The bar (E6) still applies to whichever alternative wins, which is important: N-best gives the anchor more chances to appear, but does not license firing when no alternative contains it.
- *Subsumed*: S14 (min-over-span confidence), S15 (first-occurrence-wins keying), R2 (`minConfidence`). All three become dead in N-best mode; the margin replaces them.
- *Broken*: `scoring.md:252-276` (the whole `minConfidence` section), worked example C (`:456-479`), the `-1` semantics in `KNOWN_LIMITATIONS.md:837`, and `api/command-recogniser.md`'s `minConfidence` contract. The `Documentation~` rewrite is larger than the code change.

**Expected effect.** Accuracy on the three named substitutions: **unmeasured**. Latency: **unmeasured**, expected slightly worse (N× parser cost, off the render thread, once per final; plus a wider lattice beam costs decode CPU — R2 finding 4, unmeasured on Quest). UX: a *reason* becomes available for the first time — "I heard `switch two weapons`, alternative 2 was `switch to weapons`" is displayable.

**Cost.** Engineering ~1.5–2 weeks including the doc rewrite. Runtime CPU: N parser passes per final (the parser runs once per utterance today; the selection loop is O(commands × patterns × tokens), audit §2 step 14). RAM: N transcripts. APK: zero. **On-device verification is human-only** (bridge change, per the project's verification bindings).

**Risk and kill criterion.** Risk: alternatives 2–N are near-duplicates at `--lattice-beam=2.0`, so the change buys nothing but cost; and a *wrong* alternative that parses cleanly is a new invention class of exactly the kind the bar exists to suppress. **Kill if**, on the fixture corpus, rows where an alternative supplies a command the 1-best did not do not exceed rows where an alternative *invents* a command the 1-best did not, by a clear margin.

**Cheapest validating experiment.** Zero package code. In the WSL desktop harness (`NativeBridge~/harness/main.cpp`, push mode), with a copy of `conf/model.conf` at `--lattice-beam=6.0`, call `set_max_alternatives(3)` and replay the WAV fixtures plus the augmented corpus (R6 Tier 1c). Report per row: does the correct intent appear only below rank 1 (recovery), or does a wrong intent appear only below rank 1 (invention)? Note honestly that the **699-row A/B rig cannot run this** — it stages nine parser `.cs` files and no VOSK (R6 audit), so the denominator here is the 16 fixtures until M0 lands, and a 16-case run has a 95% Wilson half-width of ±0.097 at 16/16 (R6 exec summary, measured).

**Migration.** `VoxrCommand.Confidence` keeps its field but its meaning changes; ship it as *deprecated-in-place* for one minor version alongside a new `Reliability` (M8), with `minConfidence` documented as ignored in N-best mode rather than silently bypassed. Authored assets unaffected. Add an Inspector `maxAlternatives` (0 = today's behaviour) so the change is opt-in for a release.

### M2 — Emit the grammar with weights: multiplicity as a prior, including an `[unk]` prior

**Problem.** Two documented failures the parser cannot reach because they happen before it: in-grammar substitutions where a *shorter, more frequent* grammar word beats the phrase the speaker said (`KNOWN_LIMITATIONS.md:19`, `:54`, `:71`), and noise decoding as short in-grammar words — "cough, hum, and noise can trigger false matches in grammar mode" (`:159`), whose current documented workaround is push-to-talk, i.e. a UX concession for a recognition problem.

**Mechanism.** `vosk_recognizer_new_grm` does not build a hard grammar: it feeds each JSON entry to Kaldi's `LanguageModelEstimator` (`ngram_order=2, discount=0.5`) whose `AddCounts` is documented as `count[n-gram] += 1` per sentence (R2 finding 1, read from vosk-api and Kaldi source). **Entries are counts, and repetition is weight.** `GenerateGrammarJson` (`VoxrCommandParser.cs:3076`) accumulates into `HashSet<string>` at `:3079` and emits each entry exactly once, throwing the lever away. Replace with `Dictionary<string,int>` and emit each entry `count` times. Three derived weights, none of them a new authored field in v1:
- **Phrase runs weighted above their constituent words.** `AddPhrase` (`:3182`) today adds the multi-word run *and* every word individually — deliberately, so a VAD-split utterance still decodes as fragments (comment at `:3177-3181`). Weight the run by its length (a 3-word required run emitted 3×, its words 1× each) so word order becomes a stronger bias without becoming a hard rule.
- **`[unk]` prior.** `uniqueWords.Add(UnkToken)` at `:3157` becomes `k_unk` emissions. This is the only in-decoder lever on `:159`.
- **Sibling-flat weighting (a refusal, not a boost).** The parser already computes sibling sets (`FindSiblingSets`, `:1380`). Where two entries are siblings across intents, their weights must be *equal* — otherwise the grammar acquires, at the acoustic layer, exactly the registration-order coin flip that `disambiguateSiblingTies` exists to remove (T-rules). This constraint is the innovation; naive "weight the common phrases up" would make the sibling problem worse.

**Grounding.** R2 finding 1 (`VERIFIED` from source; magnitude `UNMEASURED`). R2 rejection option A. Corroborating direction: ATCO2's contextual biasing on the identifier set was worth **53.7 points absolute** on callsign recognition (Zuluaga-Gomez et al. 2023, via R6 §9) — **measured, different domain and mechanism**, cited as evidence that identifier-side biasing has headroom, not as a predicted VoXR number.

**Code impact.** Stage 4 only, one method: `VoxrCommandParser.GenerateGrammarJson:3076-3175`, `AddPhrase:3182`, `AddSurfaceForm:3196`. No native change, no API change, no asset-format change. `GrammarManager.Rebuild` (`GrammarManager.cs:22`) calls the same static.

**Rules subsumed / kept / broken.** Subsumes none — it is upstream of every parser rule. Kept: all. **Genuine interaction to state, not a break:** S9 makes `[unk]` coverage-exempt and *transparent* (`:3881-3885`, `scoring.md:143`). Raising the `[unk]` prior makes `[unk]` more common, so more of an utterance becomes free under coverage, which weakens the coverage charge grammar-wide. That is a scoring-adjacent effect of a grammar change, and per the `grammar_ab_rig` memory a clean A/B is not by itself evidence that a scoring-adjacent change is safe.

**Expected effect.** Substitutions: **unmeasured**, mechanism is monotone but passes a hard-backoff estimator with a 0.5 discount, so it is not proportional (R2 finding 1). False triggers on noise: **unmeasured**; the mechanism cannot distinguish a cough from a quiet in-grammar word, so expect the operating point to shift, not the curve shape (R2 option A limit).

**Cost.** ~2 days of code; the work is the A/B sweep over `k_phrase` and `k_unk`. Runtime: zero (grammar JSON grows linearly; `UpdateGrammarFst` cost is paid at rebuild, which is already the audio gap). APK: zero.

**Risk and kill criterion.** The two uses pull against each other — `[unk]` mass costs in-grammar recall, and over-weighting one phrase starves its sibling. **Kill if** at every `k_unk` that measurably reduces noise-driven fires, in-grammar recall on the fixture corpus falls; **kill the phrase weighting if** any sibling pair's win rate moves at all (that is the coin flip re-entering at the acoustic layer, and it is invisible to the parser's tie machinery).

**Cheapest validating experiment.** Desktop harness, fixture corpus + `negative` audio (R6 Tier 1d), sweeping `k_phrase ∈ {1,2,3}` × `k_unk ∈ {1,3,10}`, reporting intent accuracy and false-accepts-per-hour separately. Requires the negative corpus, which does not exist (R6 §7: the project has **zero negative hours**).

**Migration.** None: identical public API, identical assets, and `k=1` reproduces today's grammar byte-for-byte after sorting. Ship the weights as internal constants first; a per-command authored `weight` is a later, separate decision (open question 6).

### M3 — Phonetic-neighbourhood matching, exact-only at the anchor, triggered by the torn posterior

**Problem.** Every named substitution is a near-homophone pair — to/two, all/fall, cease-fire/safe-five, a/on (`KNOWN_LIMITATIONS.md:19,54,71,101`) — and every OOV failure is an unpronounceable spelling: `cqb`, `pdc` → `[unk]`, with the documented workaround "spell it phonetically by hand" (`:87`). The parser compares tokens with `string.Equals(..., StringComparison.Ordinal)` (`VoxrCommandParser.cs:3592`, and ordinally in `TryMatchSlot:3671`), so a phonetically identical token is as different as an unrelated word.

**Mechanism.** Three parts, and the third is what makes it safe.
1. **Build-time G2P.** In the Editor, run `g2p_en` (CMUdict lookup + seq2seq fallback, **Apache-2.0**, R2 finding 27) over every literal, slot value and alias key of the active grammar and bake an ARPABET phone string per surface form into a table beside `_slotLookups`. Editor-only tooling; the *table* ships (a few KB), the dictionary does not.
2. **Phone-edit-distance retry on the failure path only.** When a required literal fails ordinal comparison (`:3592` else-branch) or `TryMatchSlot` returns null, retry under a weighted phone-edit distance ≤ θ. A phonetic match yields a new element outcome: `PhoneticMatchScore` (< `MatchScore`, e.g. 0.75) into both numerator and denominator, and it counts as `matchedRequired`. Costs nothing in the steady state because it runs only where the exact comparison already failed, and the DP matrix is pooled (constraint 3).
3. **Two hard restrictions, which is the design.**
   - **The anchor is exact-only.** A phonetic match may never satisfy the pattern's *first required element*. The bar (E6/E7) exists because a verb-shaped hole is unrecoverable; a *fuzzy* verb match would restore the phantom-command class the bar was shipped to kill (R2 finding 12 names this hazard explicitly). So `leadingRequiredMissed` (`:3494`, `:3611`) latches on a phonetic-only match of the first required element exactly as on a miss.
   - **A phonetic match cannot terminate another candidate's orphan run.** `IsAdmissibleStart` (`:4019`) must run its probe with phonetics disabled, or the start set widens grammar-wide and trailing coverage collapses (the same failure S11c already documents for slot-initial patterns, `scoring.md:127`).
   Optionally gate the retry on the *torn-posterior* signal: R2 finding 2 establishes that VOSK's per-word `conf` is a grammar-conditioned MBR posterior, so "two" at ≈0.50 is a **correct** report of a genuine two-way split between two words that are both in the grammar — not noise. Reading ≈0.5 as "the decoder was torn here, and the pattern says which way" is a use of confidence the package has never made.

**Grounding.** R2 finding 12 (components verified; benefit to VoXR explicitly `UNVERIFIED`), R2 finding 2 (MBR mechanism, `VERIFIED`; the 0.5-means-torn reading is an inference from mechanism and needs a red-then-green pin — remove "to" from the grammar and check whether "two"'s confidence rises). R5 §3.14 supplies the same phone-distance machinery for the authoring-time half (M6). G-SPIN's transferable half is the *discipline* — constrain to the phonetic neighbourhood first, decide semantically second (R2 finding 29; its LLM reranker is impossible offline).

**Code impact.** Stage 6, plus an Editor-only stage-4 build step. `VoxrCommandParser.cs`: `TryMatchScored:3467` (two branches), `TryMatchSlot:3671`, `IsAdmissibleStart:4019` (probe flag), the constants block (`:141-220`, one new score constant), and a new phone table beside `_slotLookups`. New Editor assembly dependency on a G2P tool; new serialized data on the slot/command assets or a sidecar asset. `MatchResult` gains a `PhoneticMatchCount` for diagnostics.

**Rules subsumed / kept / broken.**
- *Subsumed*: the hand-authored phonetic-alias workaround (`KNOWN_LIMITATIONS.md:87`) and part of the single-character-literal hazard (`:101`).
- *Kept, deliberately strengthened*: E6/E7/E8 (the bar) — the anchor becomes exact-only, which is *stricter* than today; S11/S11b/S11c (start test unchanged); A2 admission arithmetic unchanged in form.
- *Broken*: `scoring.md:29-48`'s per-element table gains a row, and A2's admission ledger now counts a partial-credit match as `matchedRequired` — which is a real weakening of the admission rule and must be stated in the doc, not buried.

**Expected effect.** **Unmeasured.** R2 finding 12 states the decision quantity precisely and says nobody has measured it: on the corpus, how many currently-failing rows does a phone-distance ≤1 substitution recover, and how many *invented commands* does it create.

**Cost.** ~2–3 weeks (G2P tooling, table format, matcher changes, and a lot of tests — `VoxrCommandParserTests.cs` is 6630 lines and every scoring rule is pinned there). Runtime CPU: only on the failure path; RAM ~KB; APK ~KB.

**Risk and kill criterion.** This makes the grammar *more* permissive at exactly the point where the project's history says permissiveness produced phantom commands. **Kill if** the recoveries-to-inventions ratio is worse than the bar's own published trade (**measured: 9 genuine commands lost against 39 invented suppressed over 699 utterances**, `scoring.md:242`) — a mechanism that reintroduces inventions faster than the bar removes them is a net loss even if recall rises.

**Cheapest validating experiment.** Text-level first, no decoder needed: take the 73 field utterances' *real* substitutions, apply the phone table offline, and count recoveries vs inventions against the pinned expectations. Then, and only then, the decoder-in-loop run. Note the trap recorded in project memory: a control on a different input proves only that a grammar can hold; only mutation proves the barred input reached the gate.

**Migration.** Additive and default-off (`phoneticTolerance = 0`). Authored assets unchanged; the phone table is generated, not authored. `KNOWN_LIMITATIONS.md:87`'s workaround section is superseded, not deleted (the alias mechanism still works).

### M4 — Positional word evidence: replace the text-keyed confidence dictionary with a token-aligned array

**Problem.** A documented, self-inflicted hazard. The per-word confidence table is built once per utterance, **keyed by word text, first occurrence wins** (`VoxrCommandParser.cs:3821-3830`, the `!ContainsKey` guard at `:3828`), so every repeat of a word scores at the first occurrence's confidence *even when that first occurrence lies outside the matched span* (S15; `scoring.md:269-274` states both consequences: a weak repeat masked by a strong earlier one, and a weak word before the match dragging the reported confidence down). It bites hardest on `NumberSequence` slots — the commands most likely to repeat a word. It also silently blocks every timing-based feature, because the parser cannot ask "which token" anything.

**Mechanism.** `ComputeConfidence` (`:4090`) takes a token-index-aligned `ReadOnlySpan<float>` (or the `VoxrWord[]` itself) instead of `Dictionary<string,float>`, and indexes it directly rather than looking up by text. Alignment comes free where `words.Length == tokens.Length` for a single VOSK result. Where the utterance buffer merged a spoken result with an injected one, the words array is shorter than the token array (`UtteranceBuffer.cs:29`; `scoring.md:276` documents exactly this case) — so track a per-segment offset in `UtteranceBuffer` and mark unaligned spans as "no data" (`-1`) rather than mis-attributing a confidence to them. That is strictly more honest than today, which happily attributes one segment's confidence to another's token.

**Grounding.** Direct code reading (`:3821-3830`, `:4090-4113`) and the documented consequences at `scoring.md:269-274`. R6 §11 supplies the reason it matters beyond correctness: ECE/NCE over the session log's `words[].confidence` cannot even be computed against per-word ground truth while the table is keyed by text. R5 §3.6 supplies the payoff: **measured**, marking low-confidence output rather than raising a threshold gave **85% vs 80%** error-detection accuracy and **11% faster** decisions (Nowrin & Vertanen, PETRA '25, n=48) — a display use of an uncalibrated number, which needs per-token confidence to render.

**Code impact.** Stage 5→6 boundary. `VoxrCommandParser.cs:3802-3838` (both `BuildWordConfidence` overloads), `:4090-4113` (`ComputeConfidence`), `VoxrCommandRecogniser.cs:719` (the call site), `UtteranceBuffer.cs:12-40` (segment offsets). Removes a `Dictionary` from the hot path, which helps the zero-allocation pin (`ZeroAllocPollPathTests.cs`, constraint 3) rather than threatening it. No public API change; the per-slot confidence already reported in the session log becomes correct.

**Rules subsumed / kept / broken.** S14 kept exactly (minimum over matched non-`[unk]` tokens). **S15 deliberately broken** — the first-occurrence rule and both of its documented consequences disappear. `scoring.md:269-274` is rewritten; worked example C's note about repeats (`:271`) goes away. The `-1` semantics (`KNOWN_LIMITATIONS.md:837`) are kept and used more, not less.

**Expected effect.** Correctness of a reported number, not accuracy of a decision — **unmeasured**, but the direction is unambiguous: the reported confidence stops being attributable to a token outside the span. Latency and CPU: neutral-to-better.

**Cost.** ≤1 week. Zero runtime cost, zero APK.

**Risk and kill criterion.** Risk: the segment-offset bookkeeping in `UtteranceBuffer` is where the bug would live, and it is exercised only when a buffer merges heterogeneous results. **Kill if** the aligned path cannot be made to agree with the text-keyed path on every existing `VoxrCommandParserTests` expectation for single-segment utterances — a disagreement there means the alignment assumption is wrong, not that the old rule was right.

**Cheapest validating experiment.** Pure PlayMode: construct an utterance repeating a word with different per-word confidences inside and outside the span, assert the reported confidence changes in the direction `scoring.md:270-272` says it does not today. This is a red-then-green pin, and it is the whole test.

**Migration.** `VoxrCommand.Confidence` values change for utterances containing repeats — a behaviour change with no API change. It must be called out in `CHANGELOG.md` as such, because a game tuned around a masked-low confidence will see commands start being rejected.

### M5 — Incremental matching on partial results, with an explicit stability and prefix-commit contract

**Problem.** Latency. The measured-by-construction budget, end of speech → `OnCommandRecognised`, is **≈545–1055 ms on the eager path and ≈2545–3055 ms on the default path**, plus an unmeasured Quest decoder catch-up the docs put at 0.5–1.0 s (R3 latency budget, from repo constants). Two stages account for 90%+ of it and both are silence timers defending against the *same* failure: the Kaldi endpointer's 500/750/1000 ms trailing-silence rules (`conf/model.conf`, `02-verified-facts.md`) and `bufferWindow` (`VoxrCommandRecogniser.cs:72`, default 0.5 s, tooltip recommends 2.0 s on Quest 3). The eager flush cannot beat the endpointer because it only ever sees finals: `HandleResult` (`:566`) is subscribed to `OnResult`, while `HandlePartialResult` (`:1331`) stores text and is compiled **only under `#if UNITY_EDITOR`** (`:503`).

**Mechanism.** Subscribe the buffer path to partials in players; enable `vosk_recognizer_set_partial_words(recognizer, 1)` (declared at `NativeBridge~/include/vosk_api.h:31`, exported per `02-verified-facts.md`) so condition 7 has real data instead of `-1`; run `TryEagerCommit` (`:4274`) over `buffer + latest partial`. The existing seven conditions plus two `Commit` extras transfer unchanged. **"Unambiguous and unextendable over a partial" needs three additional conditions, and defining them is the proposal:**

- **P-a Stability.** The identical `Commit` verdict — same intent, same pattern index, same slot values — on ≥2 consecutive *distinct* partial strings, or ≥T ms since the partial text last changed. Kaldi revises live hypotheses; VoXR's entire safety architecture was built against final transcripts.
- **P-b Monotone prefix.** The current partial must extend the previous one. A revision of already-matched tokens resets the stability counter to zero. (Partials are already de-duplicated on the bridge side — `vosk_bridge.cpp:136-140` pushes only when the string changed — so the C# side sees genuine changes.)
- **P-c Committed-prefix bookkeeping — the condition nobody else has stated.** Condition 3 ("the match reaches the end of the buffer", `scoring.md:342`) means something weaker on a partial: more audio is *certainly* coming. Committing clears the accumulated transcript (which is precisely why condition 6 exists — `scoring.md:355`), so on a partial commit the recogniser must record the number of tokens committed and **drop that many leading tokens from the subsequent final** when it arrives, or the same words parse twice and fire twice (debounce, R6, would mask it for the same intent and not for a different one). This also means `G8`'s terminality test stops being a nicety and becomes the load-bearing guarantee: on a partial, nothing but G8 stands between a commit and firing the shorter of two patterns one of which is a prefix of the other.

**Grounding.** R3 finding 3 (`VERIFIED` from this repo's code) and R3 recommendation 2. R3 finding 1: Kaldi's own `max_relative_cost` rule already does grammar-complete endpointing internally and vosk's C ABI does not expose it — VoXR's eager flush is the hand-written version of prior art inside its own decoder. R3 option B estimates **≈60–120 ms after the last word** for the commit case plus decoder catch-up — the only option in R3's list that reaches the 200–300 ms target. Corroborating patterns: unified ASR+endpointing, **measured −120 ms median / −170 ms P90** with no WER regression (Bijwadia et al., SLT 2022); two-pass endpoint verification as the shape of the stability gate (Raju et al., ASRU 2023); incremental-hypothesis instability as a *known tunable curve* — spurious edits ~90% → ~10% at 320 ms lag (Baumann et al. 2009, `UNVERIFIED` secondary summary, R5 §3.10; a shape, not a setting). R5 §3.5: perceived response time degrades at every delay level and **natural** fillers help at 4.0 s and 6.5 s while spinners do not (Maslych et al., CUI '25, n=54, measured) — so the eager path's real UX partner is an acknowledgement at first partial, not a spinner.

**Code impact.** Stages 3 and 5. `NativeBridge~/src/vosk_bridge.cpp` (one call after each recognizer construction, `:235`/`:373`) — a bridge change, therefore **on-device human verification, not claimable from WSL**. `Runtime/VoxrJsonParser.cs` (partials gain a `result` array). `VoxrCommandRecogniser.cs:503` (unconditional subscribe), `:1331` (real handler), `:592-599` (probe on partials as well as finals), plus the new committed-prefix state. `UtteranceBuffer.cs` gains a "drop N leading tokens on next append" operation. No parser change beyond three new conditions in `TryEagerCommit`.

**Rules subsumed / kept / broken.** G1–G11 all kept verbatim; three added. R20/G11 kept (skip while a pending is live). R19 kept (`EffectiveBufferWindow` may only shorten). **Broken:** `scoring.md:338-361` publishes the seven conditions as an exhaustive conjunction, with "None of the seven is implied by its neighbours" — that enumeration becomes "seven on the flush path, ten on the partial path". Audit §5 flags this as one of two closed enumerations published as exhaustive; extending it is a documented-contract change and needs the maintainer's ruling (open question 1).

**Expected effect.** Latency: **unmeasured for VoXR**; the mechanism removes both dominant terms for the commit case only — incomplete, ambiguous and pending utterances stay on the timer entirely. Accuracy: **unmeasured**, and the honest expectation is a new error class (fire on a hypothesis the final revises) traded against the split-command class.

**Cost.** ~3 weeks: the code is medium, the *tests* are the cost — `VoxrEagerCommitTests.cs` is 1913 lines against final transcripts and none of it covers revision.

**Risk and kill criterion.** The real risk is firing on a hypothesis the final contradicts, in a system whose whole safety story (the bar, coverage, sibling ties) assumes a final transcript. **Kill criterion is measurable before any code ships:** if the fraction of utterances on which some partial would have produced a `Commit` verdict that the final contradicts is non-trivial, this proposal is dead and the answer is the endpointer/VAD path instead (R3 options A+C).

**Cheapest validating experiment.** R3 recommendation 2, read-only: **do not implement it — instrument it.** Log, per fixture, the sequence of partials and the `TryEagerCommit` verdict each *would* have produced, and compare against the final's verdict. One rig change, no Unity, no device, and it produces exactly the number the kill criterion needs.

**Migration.** Gate behind the existing `eagerFlushOnCompleteMatch` plus a new `eagerFlushOnPartials` (default off), so the shipped default path is unchanged. `api/speech-recogniser.md:56`'s `OnPartialResult` contract is unaffected; `command-recognition.md`'s eager-flush section and `scoring.md` §6 both need the three new conditions.

### M6 — Authoring-time confusability linting against the real decoder, plus a vocabulary gate

**Problem.** All five construction-time validation passes are string-shape checks (audit §5.7): casing, punctuation, single characters, prefix-droppable literals, sibling discriminators, duplicate intents. Not one of them can see an *acoustic* hazard, so `switch to weapons`/`switch two weapons`, `all`/`fall` and `cease fire`/`safe five` were all discovered in the field. Worse, an authored word absent from the model vocabulary is dropped **silently**: `UpdateGrammarFst` emits a `KALDI_WARN` per unknown token and `vosk_set_log_level(-1)` at `NativeBridge~/src/vosk_bridge.cpp:215` suppresses it, so authoring `cqb` produces no signal from any layer of the system (R2, `VERIFIED`) and the failure surfaces only as `[unk]` on device (`KNOWN_LIMITATIONS.md:87`).

**Mechanism.** Two tiers plus a gate.
- **Gate (hours of work, highest value per hour in this report).** Add `vosk_bridge_find_word(const char*)` to the C ABI wrapping `vosk_model_find_word` (declared `NativeBridge~/include/vosk_api.h:21`, exported per `02-verified-facts.md`), and run every entry of `GenerateGrammarJson`'s output through it at `Configure` time. Raise a **named Editor error** — "the word `cqb` is not in the model vocabulary and will be dropped from the grammar" — and, where M3's G2P is present, propose the spelled alias automatically instead of telling the developer to invent one.
- **Tier 1 — cheap phonetic lint (Editor, at parser construction).** Weighted phone-edit distance across every pair of pattern literal runs and slot values belonging to *different* intents; warn below a threshold. This is the affordable version of Microsoft's Grammar Confusability Metric (US7844456B2), whose own method perturbs acoustic feature vectors and re-recognises (R5 finding 13).
- **Tier 2 — real-decoder lint (offline, Editor button or CI).** Render every pattern (and every slot value in context) through the existing TTS fixture pipeline (`Tests~/Fixtures/generate.py`), replay through the real decoder via the push-audio harness, and report per pattern: the decoded transcript, which required element was dropped or substituted, whether the parse yields the intended intent — and, separately, **whether the pattern's first required element survived**, because that is the bar's input and a pattern whose anchor the decoder reliably drops is silently unusable. That last column is the one no existing tool produces.

**Grounding.** R2 recommendation 2 (`VERIFIED`: symbol exported, warning suppressed, ~30-line change). R5 §3.14 (`VERIFIED`): the confusability metric patent, Alexa's Utterance Conflict Detection running **on every model build**, and the NLU Evaluation Tool over annotation sets — three shipped DX products VoXR is one step from, because it already has `InjectText`, a WAV fixture corpus, an A/B rig and five warnings. R5 also supplies the failure mode: a warning that fires on half a real grammar is noise and will be ignored, so the threshold must be tuned against a corpus where ground truth exists.

**Code impact.** Stage 4 and stage 8. New C export in `NativeBridge~/src/vosk_bridge.cpp` + `BridgeNative.cs` binding (bridge change → device verification human-only, though the *Editor* path via `VoxrNative.cs` can be verified in WSL/Windows). New `Editor/` window and a `Tools~`-style offline script. A sixth construction-time warning appended at the end of the constructor's warning block — note the ordering constraint recorded at `VoxrCommandParser.cs:701-710`: `VoxrCommandRecogniserInjectionTests` queues *ordered* log expectations, so a new scan must be appended, never inserted.

**Rules subsumed / kept / broken.** Subsumes nothing at runtime. Complements `WarnOnDroppableRequiredLiteral` (`:868`) and `WarnOnSiblingDiscriminator` (`:1644`), both of which reason about *dropped* words structurally and neither of which can see a *substituted* one. Breaks nothing; adds warnings, which per `KNOWN_LIMITATIONS.md:308` already re-emit on every set switch — the new ones must be `[Conditional("UNITY_EDITOR")]`, unlike `RunValidationWarnings` (`:732`, the one pass that is not, and which therefore logs in shipped players).

**Expected effect.** Developer UX: converts three field-discovered failures into author-time warnings — **unmeasured** whether the linter would actually have caught those three, which is exactly what the validating experiment asks. Accuracy: zero direct effect; it changes what developers ship.

**Cost.** Gate: ~2 days. Tier 1: ~1 week (needs M3's phone table). Tier 2: ~2 weeks, and it *is* the decoder-in-the-loop harness R6 recommendation 4 asks for, so its cost is shared.

**Risk and kill criterion.** **Kill Tier 1 if** its threshold cannot be set so that the demo grammar (`Tests~/Runtime/DemoGrammar.cs`) produces few enough warnings to be read — a linter nobody reads is worse than none because it teaches developers to ignore the console.

**Cheapest validating experiment.** Retrodiction. Run Tier 1 and Tier 2 over a grammar reconstructed to contain the three known field failures and check that each is flagged, then run both over the demo grammar and count false alarms. Zero device time; the fixture corpus already contains a `homophone` category.

**Migration.** Purely additive. `KNOWN_LIMITATIONS.md:87`'s "spell it phonetically by hand" workaround becomes "the Editor tells you, and proposes the spelling".

### M7 — Anchored `NumberSequence` slots: stop a digit grammar from disabling coverage globally

**Problem.** A documented, grammar-wide failure with a narrow cause. `CanStartPattern` (`VoxrCommandParser.cs:4064`) consults `_startSlots`, and a pattern beginning with an open-ended `NumberSequence` makes **almost every token a legal pattern start**, so almost nothing is ever charged as an orphan *anywhere in that grammar* (S11c; `scoring.md:127`; `KNOWN_LIMITATIONS.md:402`). One authored pattern therefore switches off the trailing half of coverage for every command — and coverage is the mechanism that keeps a bare pattern from out-ranking its slot-filled sibling and discarding an argument (`scoring.md:419`). Separately, digits are the vocabulary where "two" sits pinned at ≈0.50 confidence, forcing `minConfidence` below 0.5 permanently (`KNOWN_LIMITATIONS.md:121`).

**Mechanism.** Type the slot rather than treating all slots alike.
- A `NumberSequence` slot declares an **anchor requirement**: it may begin a pattern only if the author explicitly opts in (`{heading!}` or a bool on `VoxrSlotAsset`). Otherwise the digit vocabulary is *not* added to `_startSlots` during the start-set build (`:576-614`), and `CanStartPattern` stops answering yes at every digit. Trailing coverage is restored grammar-wide, at the cost of one authoring decision.
- The digit vocabulary becomes a **sub-grammar** rather than a flat dump: today `GenerateGrammarJson:3137-3145` adds every digit word as an individual entry as soon as any `NumberSequence` slot exists anywhere. Under M2's weighting, emit digits as *runs* anchored to their introducing literal (`heading two seven zero`, `track one two four four`) so the bigram estimator learns that digits follow anchors, which is what makes a stray "four" after a complete heading expensive rather than free (`command-recognition.md`'s worked hazard: `orient heading two seven zero four` absorbed as `elevation`).
- **Homophone-class typing at digit positions.** Combined with M3's phone table: at a digit position, a token in the same homophone class as the expected digit (to/two, for/four, ate/eight, won/one) resolves toward the *slot's* type, because the grammar says a number belongs there. This is the same "the pattern says which way the decoder was torn" idea as M3, applied where it is most needed.

**Grounding.** `scoring.md:126-128` and `KNOWN_LIMITATIONS.md:402` state the failure and its grammar-wide reach. R2 finding 2 establishes that a ≈0.50 confidence on "two" is a correct posterior over a genuine two-way in-grammar split, not unreliability — so typing the position is a legitimate tie-break, not wishful thinking. R6 §13 supplies the general form: not all words are equal, and VoXR's per-element costs are already an implicit statement of that.

**Code impact.** Stage 4 and 6. `VoxrCommandParser.cs:576-614` (start-set build), `:4064` (`CanStartPattern`), `:3137-3145` (digit emission), `TryMatchNumberSequence` (slot match). `VoxrSlotAsset.cs:14` gains a field (additive to a ScriptableObject; `VoxrSlotDefinition` is a public readonly struct — adding a field is source-compatible, changing one is not, audit §6.5).

**Rules subsumed / kept / broken.** S11/S11b kept. **S11c changes**: its second documented consequence (slot-initial patterns weaken coverage grammar-wide) becomes opt-in rather than automatic. `scoring.md:127` and `KNOWN_LIMITATIONS.md:402` are rewritten. E1/E2/A4 untouched. Nothing about the bar changes.

**Expected effect.** Restores trailing coverage in grammars that currently have none — **unmeasured** how many real grammars that is, though the maintainer's own `{heading}`/`{track}` commands are exactly the shape. Effect on the digit-confidence floor: **unmeasured**.

**Cost.** ~1 week, plus the asset-migration question. Zero runtime cost (the start set gets *smaller*, so `IsAdmissibleStart`'s sweep gets cheaper).

**Risk and kill criterion.** Risk: a grammar that genuinely needs a slot-initial number pattern silently stops matching it after upgrade. Mitigation is that the opt-in must default to *today's* behaviour on deserialization and the Editor must warn once. **Kill if** the opt-in default cannot be made backward-compatible without a serialized-field migration hazard of the kind `KNOWN_LIMITATIONS.md:365` already documents for `[FormerlySerializedAs]` and prefab overrides.

**Cheapest validating experiment.** Text-level, on the A/B rig as it exists: construct a grammar with and without a slot-initial `NumberSequence` and measure how many of the 699 rows change score. The rig can answer this one without a decoder, because it is purely a parser rule.

**Migration.** Serialized default preserves current behaviour; the Editor warns when a slot-initial `NumberSequence` is disabling coverage and names the cost. `CHANGELOG.md` under `[Unreleased]`; `scoring.md` §2 and `KNOWN_LIMITATIONS.md:402` updated.

### M8 — The probabilistic reformulation: learn the element costs, keep the structure, and ship the calibrated score before it gates anything

**Problem.** Four scoring constants and two thresholds are hand-set, and every one of them is a claim about a probability nobody estimated: `RequiredLiteralMissPenalty = 0f` (`:187`), `RequiredSlotMissPenalty = -1.0f` (`:143`), `OptionalLiteralScore = 0.5f` (`:142`), `DefaultCoverageWeight = 1.0f` (`:203`), `minScore = 0.6` (`VoxrCommandRecogniser.cs:31`), `minConfidence = 0.4` (`:27`). Both thresholds are pinned: `minConfidence` cannot go above ~0.5 because "two" sits there (`KNOWN_LIMITATIONS.md:121`), and `minScore` cannot go above ~0.7 without making three-element patterns demand a perfect transcript (`scoring.md:288`). And the costs are *uniform over words*: a dropped `to` and a dropped `launch` cost identically, though the decoder drops them at wildly different rates.

**Mechanism, and the explicit verdict on the wholesale rewrite.**

*Accepted — the tractable half.* Two independent changes, in this order.
1. **Word-class-conditioned miss costs.** Replace the single `RequiredLiteralMissPenalty` with a per-surface-form deletion cost estimated from the decoder's *measured* deletion rate on that word — obtained by `sclite` alignment over the fixture corpus (R6 §6: SCTK's `sclite` + `sc_stats -t mapsswe`, NIST open licence). A word VOSK drops in 40% of renditions is weak evidence of absence; a word it essentially never drops is strong evidence. The score keeps its ratio form, its dynamic denominator, and every selection key; only the constants become a small table. Same for substitutions: the confusion pairs `sclite` mines are exactly the table M3 needs.
2. **A calibrated `VoxrCommand.Reliability`, reported and gating nothing.** A logistic regression over 4–6 features the parser already computes — score, coverage charge, admission margin (`MatchedRequired − MissedRequired`), min-conf or N-best margin (M1), span fraction, leading-miss flag — with coefficients shipped as a serialized asset so a game can re-fit. Emitted on `VoxrCommand` and in the session log. It becomes a *gate* only if it beats the two thresholds on held-out data, in a later release.

*Rejected for now — the wholesale noisy-channel rewrite* (`P(command) × P(transcript | command)` with learned deletion/substitution costs replacing the whole rule set). The inventory the maintainer needs:
- **What it would subsume**: S1 (the ratio), S2–S5 (per-element credit and the dynamic denominator), S6–S7 (both miss costs → deletion log-probs), S8/S13 (coverage → an insertion-run probability), S14/S15 (confidence → a per-token emission term), A1 (the score>0 floor), R1/R2 (two thresholds → one posterior threshold), and the three worked examples in `scoring.md` §7 as *derivations* rather than rules.
- **What it must keep as hard constraints, not learned terms**: **E6/E6b/E7/E8/E11 — the leading-required-miss bar and everything downstream of a barred round** (a maintainer ruling, shipped in 2.0.0 after a phantom command executed a maneuver order in the field; a probability model will cheerfully fire a 0.7-posterior verb-less command, and `scoring.md:215` states exactly why no arithmetic reaches this: "`minScore` sees 2/3 and cannot ask *which* third went missing"); **R4 completeness** (a missing required *slot* is a contract failure — the handler will not receive its argument — not a low probability); **A4 key 1 and the determinism of selection** (`scoring.md:7` publishes the parser as deterministic and games are built on it); **T1–T15** (sibling disambiguation: where the discriminating word was never uttered, no model recovers it — an information-theoretic wall, R2 "what this area cannot fix"); **S9's `[unk]` exemption** (a decoder artefact, not a probability).
- **What published numbers it invalidates**: `scoring.md:29-48` (the element table), `:88` (the formula), `:96-104` (coverage semantics), `:166-180` (selection keys 2–4), `:246-289` (both gates), and the five named 699-utterance measurements at `:178`, `:238`, `:242`, `:454`, `:511` — none of which survives a change to the score they were measured under. Also `api/command-recogniser.md`'s `minScore`/`minConfidence` contracts and the entire "Reading a session log" diagnosis table (`:545-559`), which is written in terms of `matched / elements` arithmetic.
- **Why it is rejected now, not on principle**: the fitting data is 699 rows *derived* from 16 committed transcripts by four mechanical perturbation families with no decoder in the loop, plus 73 field utterances from **one speaker** with no retained audio (R6 audit). Under Dwork et al. (*Science* 2015, R6 §12) all three corpora are now part of the parser's *training* procedure, not a test set. R6's power calculation: detecting 90%→95% needs **≈435 utterances per arm unpaired or ≈290 paired**, and **fewer than 6 discordant utterances in one direction is never significant** (measured/derived). A rewrite that invalidates every published number, justified on a corpus that cannot certify a 5-point change, is not a trade worth making until `human-core` exists.

**Grounding.** R2 finding 11 (Qiu et al. ICASSP 2021; Li et al. ICASSP 2022; Yu, Li & Deng TASLP 2011) — the consistent finding is that a recogniser's native score is a poor confidence and a small *post-hoc* estimator over hand-designed features beats it, applied without modifying the engine; and Li et al. 2022 names VoXR's exact hazard, that an estimator fitted on TTS will be miscalibrated on Quest-mic speech. R2 recommendation 5 says explicitly: ship it as a reported number gating nothing. R6 §11 supplies the scoreboard (NCE, ECE over 10 equal-width bins) and notes the finding-of-record possibility: if grammar-mode `conf` has near-zero NCE, `minConfidence` is a dead knob and every tuning decision that leaned on it was noise. R6 §13 supplies the word-class argument, including the inversion that matters here — at *utterance/dialog-act* level non-content words mattered more than named entities, which matches VoXR's own field finding that dropped short function words are its most damaging error class.

**Code impact.** Stage 6. Part 1: the constants block (`VoxrCommandParser.cs:141-220`) becomes a small table plus a lookup in `TryMatchScored:3467`'s two required-miss branches; the parser gains a cost-table parameter alongside `_coverageWeight` (`:203`) and `_minScore` (`:414`). Part 2: a new `Reliability` field on `VoxrCommand` (`VoxrCommand.cs:33` — additive), a ~30-float coefficient asset, and a session-log schema bump (v3) which R6 already recommends for `barred` and `runnerUpIntent`/`runnerUpScore`.

**Rules subsumed / kept / broken.** As enumerated above. In the *accepted* scope specifically: S6 and S7's **uniformity** is subsumed (the constants become word-conditioned); their *structure* and the whole of A1–A8, E1–E13, T1–T15, G1–G11 are kept. Broken: `scoring.md:36-38,47` (the miss-cost prose becomes "a table, not a constant") and `:63-79`'s "short patterns are disproportionately fragile" worked table, whose arithmetic changes.

**Expected effect.** **Unmeasured throughout.** The only measured anchors are external: post-hoc calibration produces "significant NCE increase and EER reduction" in the canonical reference (Yu, Li & Deng 2011, tables `UNVERIFIED`), and word-level confidence models report NCE 0.4 / ECE 0.05 in a modern system (Wang et al., ASRU 2021, R6 §11) — neither transfers to a Kaldi grammar decoder.

**Cost.** Part 1: ~1.5 weeks plus a `sclite` integration (half a day per R6 §6). Part 2: ~1 week of code, and the labelling/fitting harness is where the real time goes. Runtime: a logistic regression is ~30 floats and a dot product — free.

**Risk and kill criterion.** The dominant risk is Li et al. 2022's: a calibrator fitted on 16 TTS renders from one Piper voice plus 73 utterances from one human will be calibrated *for its author*. **Kill part 2 if** it does not beat the current two-threshold rule on AUC and ECE on a held-out split of 699 rows — R2's own formulation: if it cannot beat two thresholds on 699 rows it will not beat them in the field. **Kill part 1 if** the deletion-rate table estimated from TTS does not correlate with the deletions observed in the 73 field utterances; TTS deletion rates for function words are exactly where the synthetic/real gap is worst (R6 §2/§3: **+6.3 pp to +18.7 pp**, architecture-dependent, measured).

**Cheapest validating experiment.** Entirely offline, zero shipping change: dump per-attempt features from the existing session logs and A/B outputs, label each row from the existing pins, fit a 4-feature logistic regression, and report AUC and ECE against the current two-threshold rule on a held-out split. For part 1, run `sclite` over the fixture corpus and simply *look at* the substitution table before writing any code — if it does not contain to/two, all/fall and cease/safe, the premise is wrong.

**Migration.** `Reliability` is additive and gates nothing in v1. `minScore`/`minConfidence` remain, documented as legacy once `Reliability` gates. The cost table ships with defaults reproducing today's constants exactly, so a game that does not opt in sees no change — that reproduction is the acceptance test.

### M9 — Deictic slots bound to gaze by word timing

**Problem.** Not a failure the parser has; a class of utterance it cannot express. Every target must be named (`hotel one`, `alpha three`), so the grammar grows with the game's object count and each utterance carries more words to misrecognise. The information needed to say "fire at *that*" already exists in the payload and is thrown away: VOSK emits per-word `start`/`end` (`vosk_recognizer_set_words(1)` at `vosk_bridge.cpp:235`, parsed at `VoxrJsonParser.cs:52`), and **only `Text` and `Confidence` reach the parser** — timing is never consulted by any scoring rule (audit §3.2).

**Mechanism.** A pattern element suffix marks a slot position as deixis-capable (`{target*}`). The grammar gains ~10 closed-class words (`it, them, that, this, those, these, one, him, her, they`) — trivial beside the per-value entries, and in exchange **the slot's value list can shrink**, so the grammar gets smaller, not bigger. When the matcher lands a pronoun at that position it calls an `IVoxrDeixisResolver` delegate (the second interface in `Runtime/`, after M1's) with the *word's own* start/end times, and the resolver picks from a per-category salience ring buffer over the window `[word.start − 1.0 s, word.end + 1.0 s]`, weighting by proximity to the word — **not** by gaze at the moment the command fired, which can be 2 s late because of `bufferWindow`. The resolver substitutes a registered value before delivery, so the invariant that a delivered slot value is a registered value holds. A resolver that cannot decide returns a confidence and the tie routes into the existing pending/ambiguity path (T-rules reused verbatim) rather than picking.

**Grounding.** R5 §3.1 (`VERIFIED`): gaze + laser pointing + scene metadata added to a VR speech transcript gave **+26.5% coreference-resolution accuracy** over speech alone (Bovo et al. 2025, 12 participants — measured, GPT-based resolver, not this mechanism). R5 §3.2 (`VERIFIED`): speech+raycast beat a spatial-UI baseline at 4 targets — **19.8 s vs 35.9 s** search, **12.5 s vs 23.3 s** repeat, TLX 4.06 vs 4.94, n=24, 2592 trials — while at *one* target the baseline was faster, which bounds the claim. R5 §3.13 (`UNVERIFIED`, secondary summaries): gesture overlapped speech in 95% of multimodal constructions, intermodal lags ~0.3–0.8 s, ~1 s a sufficient fusion threshold — the source of the ±1.0 s window, which must be confirmed against VoXR's own field logs before being hardcoded. R5 also names the hazard precisely: a wrong referent is a *silent* wrong action, the same class the bar was shipped to stop.

**Code impact.** Stages 4–6. Requires **M4 first** — the parser cannot ask "when was this token spoken" while its only word evidence is a text-keyed dictionary. Then: `VoxrCommandParser` pattern parsing (`ExtractSlotName:4115`, `IsOptionalSlot:4127`) gains the suffix; `TryMatchSlot:3671` gains a deixis branch; `GenerateGrammarJson:3076` adds the pronoun set; `VoxrCommandRecogniser` gains resolver registration alongside `RegisterSlotValueProvider` (`:330`), the only existing delegate extension point. `UtteranceBuffer` must carry timings across merged segments (same bookkeeping M4 introduces).

**Rules subsumed / kept / broken.** Everything kept; deixis resolves *inside* slot matching, so scoring, coverage, admission, the bar and the tie machinery see a normal matched slot. The **documented hazard to restate**: a word the decoder dropped leaves no timing hole (field-verified, `00-brief.md` §3), so timings are trustworthy only for words that were actually emitted — a deictic resolution can never be inferred from a *gap*.

**Expected effect.** Grammar size falls (mechanical, unmeasured magnitude). Utterance length falls, which reduces exposure to substitution — **unmeasured for VoXR**. UX: the multi-target case is where voice wins (measured above); the single-target case is where it does not.

**Cost.** ~3 weeks after M4. Runtime: O(small N) over a ring buffer, main-thread, allocation-free — consistent with the parser's style and the zero-alloc pin.

**Risk and kill criterion.** A wrong referent fires a *correct-looking* command at the wrong object — worse than a rejection, and invisible in every existing diagnostic. **Kill if** the resolver cannot be made to abstain (route to pending) at a rate that keeps wrong-referent firings below the phantom rate the bar currently achieves; a deixis feature that reintroduces silent wrong actions undoes 2.0.0.

**Cheapest validating experiment.** Not an accuracy experiment — a *plumbing* one, because the plumbing is the risk. Thread timings through `UtteranceBuffer` → parser and assert, on the WAV fixtures, that the token→time mapping survives a VAD-split utterance merged from two finals. If it does not survive that, the feature is unbuildable as specified. Only then prototype the resolver in the host project against head-gaze.

**Migration.** Additive: the suffix is new syntax, so existing `patterns` strings are unaffected (audit §6.5 warns that any change to *element semantics* silently reinterprets existing assets — a new suffix on a new position does not). `IVoxrDeixisResolver` is a new public interface; no existing member changes.

## Roadmap

**M0 — prerequisite, and nothing above is certifiable without it.** Three items, none of them a matching-layer change, all of them blocking:
- **Put a decoder in the A/B loop** (R6 recommendation 4). `stage.sh` stages nine parser `.cs` files and zero VOSK, so *every* number the project has produced about scoring is a statement about the parser conditioned on a transcript distribution. M1, M2, M3, M6-tier-2 and M8-part-1 are all decoder-side or decoder-measured and are currently unmeasurable. ~3 days of plumbing (the push-audio bridge mode already exists, unused from C# — `BridgeNative.cs:53,60`).
- **Log `barred` and `runnerUpIntent`/`runnerUpScore`** (R6, just below its cut). Two session-log fields. Today a barred round leaves no trace at all (E11) and `rejectReason: no match` conflates "nothing matched" with "everything matched and was barred" — so the log cannot measure the cost of the rule 2.0.0 shipped on, and cannot supply the near-miss distribution M8's fit needs.
- **Adopt CHiME-4 dual reporting** (R6 recommendation 2): every accuracy claim reports (TTS number, human number) and rules on the human number; rename the 16 fixtures and 699 rows to *development* sets in the docs. Costs a policy sentence.

**Phase 1 — ≤1 week each, no evidence prerequisite, ship in any order.**
- **M4** (positional word evidence). Self-contained, fixes a documented hazard, and is a hard prerequisite for M9 and for any per-word confidence display.
- **M6 gate** (`vosk_model_find_word` vocabulary check). ~2 days; converts a documented mystery into a named Editor error.
- **M7** (anchored `NumberSequence`). Parser-only; measurable on the existing text rig without M0.
- **M2** behind a default-off flag (weighted grammar emission). The *code* is 2 days; the sweep waits on M0 and on a negative corpus.

**Phase 2 — needs M0, 2–3 weeks each, run in this dependency order.**
- **M1** (N-best). Depends on M0 for its kill criterion and on a `--lattice-beam` decision. Its confidence replacement supersedes part of M4's payload, so land M4 first and let M1 extend it rather than replace it.
- **M6 tiers 1–2** (confusability linter). Tier 2 *is* M0's harness pointed at authored patterns instead of fixtures, so its marginal cost is small once M0 exists. Tier 1 needs M3's phone table.
- **M3** (phonetic matching). Depends on the G2P table shared with M6 tier 1. Highest risk in Phase 2; its kill criterion is a ratio measured against the bar's published 9-vs-39 trade.

**Phase 3 — multi-week redesigns, each with a real chance of being killed by its own experiment.**
- **M5** (partials). Instrument-only first; the go/no-go is a contradiction rate measured before any code ships. Independent of M1/M3 but competes with them for the same review bandwidth, and its doc impact (an "exhaustive" published enumeration) needs a maintainer ruling first.
- **M8 part 1** (word-class costs) after `sclite` mining; **M8 part 2** (reported `Reliability`) after M1 supplies the margin feature. Neither should gate anything until `human-core` exists.
- **M9** (deixis) after M4. The largest UX win in the reports and the one with the most external measured support, but it is a new silent-error surface and belongs last.

**What needs the evaluation harness first:** M1, M2, M3, M6-tier-2, M8 (both parts). **What does not:** M4, M6-gate, M7, and M5's instrumentation step. **What needs a corpus the project does not have:** M2's `[unk]` prior and any false-accept claim need ≥5 hours of negative audio (R6 §7 — the project has zero negative hours); M8's calibration and any ranking between two of these proposals needs `human-core`, 10 speakers × 30 phrases × 3 conditions, which R6 calls the single highest-leverage item in its report and **the only one Claude cannot do**.

## What to stop doing

1. **Stop treating `minConfidence` as a noise gate.** It is a grammar-conditioned MBR posterior — it answers "given that the speaker said something this grammar can represent, which word was it", and is confidently wrong for a cough (R2 finding 2, `VERIFIED` from vosk-api source; corroborated by vosk-api issue #741, an unrelated user reporting VoXR's exact symptom at confidence ≈1.0). `KNOWN_LIMITATIONS.md:159` offers push-to-talk as the workaround for cough-triggered false matches; that is a UX concession standing in for a rejection mechanism, and no value of `minConfidence` will ever be it.
2. **Stop deduplicating the grammar.** `HashSet<string>` at `VoxrCommandParser.cs:3079` discards multiplicity, and multiplicity is the only free biasing lever VOSK offers (R2 finding 1, `VERIFIED` from `LanguageModelEstimator::AddCounts`).
3. **Stop suppressing the decoder's vocabulary warnings.** `vosk_set_log_level(-1)` at `NativeBridge~/src/vosk_bridge.cpp:215` hides `"Ignoring word missing in vocabulary"` for every OOV grammar entry, which is why `cqb` fails silently at authoring time and loudly on device.
4. **Stop keying per-word confidence by word text.** `VoxrCommandParser.cs:3828`'s `!ContainsKey` guard produces two documented wrong answers (`scoring.md:269-274`) and blocks every timing-based feature.
5. **Stop reporting "N of 699 rows changed" as evidence.** The rows are dependent (all 699 descend from 16 committed transcripts via four mechanical families), so a count of changed rows has no inferential content and no off-the-shelf test applies to it as written (R6 audit; R6 Tier 3 item 8). The replacement is exact McNemar on per-utterance semantic accuracy with a session/voice-blocked bootstrap, and the bracing floor that **fewer than 6 discordant utterances in one direction is never significant**.
6. **Stop measuring parser changes against a decoder held constant by construction.** The A/B rig has no VOSK in it (R6 audit, `grep -rn 'vosk'` over both rig directories returns nothing). Six of the seven failure modes in `00-brief.md` §3 are decoder-side; text injection covers roughly one seventh of the observed problem space.
7. **Stop paying twice for the same mid-command split.** The Kaldi endpointer is set conservatively (0.5/0.75/1.0 s trailing silence, `conf/model.conf`) *and* `bufferWindow` is set conservatively (2.0 s recommended on Quest 3), both defending against the same failure. R3's one-line conclusion: two stages account for 90%+ of controllable latency, both are silence timers, and paying for it twice is the design defect. Note the correction in `02-verified-facts.md`: `set_endpointer_delays` is **not** exported by the shipped `libvosk.so`, so the endpointer half is a `conf/model.conf` edit, not a runtime call — R3's finding 2 is wrong on that point and R1 is right.
8. **Stop growing the grammar one slot value per game object.** Deixis makes the grammar *smaller* while making the utterances shorter (R5 §3.1).
9. **Stop answering acoustic hazards with string-shape warnings.** Five of the six construction-time passes (audit §5.7) reason about spelling and structure; none of the three named field failures is visible to any of them.
10. **Stop reading a green fixture run as acceptance evidence.** Sixteen cases from one Piper voice give a 95% Wilson half-width of ±0.097 at 16/16 and ±0.163 at 14/16 (R6, measured) — the corpus cannot resolve any accuracy difference smaller than about 15 points, and under adaptive-data-analysis (Dwork et al. 2015) it has been the *training* set for the scoring rules since #31.

## Open questions for the maintainer

1. **Is `Documentation~/scoring.md` a contract or a description?** Two enumerations are published as exhaustive — `OnUnrecognisedSpeech`'s fire/silent set (`:309-321`) and `TryEagerCommit`'s seven conditions (`:338-361`). M5 extends the second. Which of the two may grow, and does growing one require a major version?
2. **Is the bar's uniformity (E7) negotiable given *positive* evidence?** Today it is positional and absolute: a winner whose first required element matched nothing fires nothing, "whatever it scored". M1 and M3 can both supply evidence the anchor *was* spoken (it appears in alternative 2; it is one phone away from the token that was heard). Does the bar stay absolute, or does it become "no evidence of the anchor from any source"? This is the only route back to the 9 genuine commands per 699 the bar costs (`scoring.md:242`) — and it is a maintainer ruling, not a design decision.
3. **One number or two?** M8 proposes shipping `Reliability` as a *reported* value alongside `minScore`/`minConfidence` for at least one release. Is a two-lifetime overlap acceptable, or must a replacement replace?
4. **Will you take a second native dependency?** Nothing in this report requires one (M1 uses the shipped binary; M3's G2P is Editor-only). A VAD or a text-enrolled spotter — the only credible answers to the cough/hum entry — would mean ONNX Runtime in `NativeBridge~`, ~15 MB of `libonnxruntime.so` before a model byte (R1).
5. **Will you record `human-core`?** 10 speakers × 30 phrases × 3 conditions, half sealed at generation time (R6 recommendation 1). The entire evidence base today is two speakers, one of them synthetic. Every ranking between the proposals above is uncertifiable without it, and it cannot be delegated.
6. **Should grammar weights be authored or derived?** M2 derives them structurally. A per-command `weight` on `VoxrCommandAsset` is more powerful and is a new tuning surface a game developer would have to face — the same objection R3 raises against per-keyword spotter thresholds.
7. **Is `[unk]`'s coverage exemption (S9) still right if `[unk]` becomes common?** M2's `[unk]` prior makes out-of-grammar audio land on a token that coverage charges nothing for and that is transparent rather than a run terminator. The exemption was designed when `[unk]` was rare.
8. **Should a command be able to fire before the speaker finishes the order?** M5 makes that possible for complete unambiguous commands. In a bridge-command game "the officer waits until you finish" may be a design property rather than a latency bug — and R5 §3.5 offers the cheaper alternative: acknowledge at first partial and execute later, which makes 2 s of silence read as intentional (measured: natural fillers significantly improved perceived response time at 4.0 s and 6.5 s; spinners did not).
9. **What is the acceptable Editor-side size and licence cost of a G2P dictionary?** CMUdict-based `g2p_en` is Apache-2.0 (R2 finding 27), but it is a generation-time tool whose output ships and whose input does not.
10. **Does R4 exist?** It was unavailable when this was written. If it covers speaker adaptation, note that the shipped model uses online i-vectors (`ivector/` present, `02-verified-facts.md`) and that `vosk_recognizer_set_spk_model` is exported but never called — a personalization surface none of the proposals above touches.
