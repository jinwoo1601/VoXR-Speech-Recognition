# Design: Joining the Push-Audio Harness to the Parser Rig

**Branch:** `design-joined-rig`
**Origin:** next-level plan item R1 (`03-critique-and-ranking.md` rank 4; Phase C).
**Status:** **G1 LOCKED 2026-10-13; re-locked at G1 2026-10-13 with Amendment A1 (adr-0006).** Rulings in adr-0001 through adr-0006. This document is immutable from here; a contradiction found during implementation reopens it on a new design branch and re-locks.

**Scope, agreed at G0 (2026-10-13):** join the WSL push-audio harness (real audio through the real decoder) to the parser A/B rig (the real parser) with a pipe, so one run goes audio → decoder → parser → scored outcome; replace the exact-equality comparison at `VoxrBatchTestRunner.cs:208` and `:303-329` (the research cited `:230-233`, a comment) with a scorer that gives slot partial credit; define a run-output format that the gated items consume; state how the standing rule "re-pin, never edit" (rulings 2 and 10) applies to the acoustic baselines, so that `expectations.json` and the WAV-replay expectations are regenerated by running the harness and never hand-edited; and have the rig accept the manifests of the future R5 human corpus and R4 negative corpus without building either. The kill criterion: if the joined rig cannot reproduce the harness's committed `expectations.json` baseline byte for byte on the 16 fixtures, the plumbing is wrong and no number from it may be quoted.

> **What's decided going in.** The human (the maintainer) ruled at G0 on 2026-10-13: the work runs on the **design track**, branch `design-joined-rig`, with the scope above agreed as proposed. The item is needed because the two instruments already exist but the pipe between them and a scorer with slot partial credit do not: the push-audio harness already replays real audio through the real decoder (`NativeBridge~/harness/main.cpp:1-8`), the parser rig already stages the real parser (`stage.sh:15-25`), and the current comparison is exact equality (`VoxrBatchTestRunner.cs:208` and `:303-329` (the research cited `:230-233`, a comment)). It gates R10, R11, R12, R13, R14's acceptance, R21, R22 and both R41 screens — everything decoder-, grammar- or DSP-side. The kill criterion: if the joined rig cannot reproduce the harness's committed `expectations.json` baseline byte for byte on the 16 fixtures, the plumbing is wrong and no number from it may be quoted.
>
> **Non-goals:** any decoder, DSP or grammar change (R11, R14, R15 and the rest are later items gated on this one); building the R5 human corpus or the R4 negative corpus; the Phase B probes (a separate lab, run concurrently as `lab-next-level-probes`); ranking two decoders on TTS-only audio (standing rule, critique C12) (see §0).

## 0. Scope (agreed at G0)

**In**

1. The pipe from the WSL push-audio harness (real audio → real decoder) into the parser A/B rig (real parser), so one run goes audio → decoder → parser → scored outcome.
2. A scorer with slot partial credit, replacing the exact-equality comparison at `VoxrBatchTestRunner.cs:208` and `:303-329` (the research cited `:230-233`, a comment).
3. A run-output format that the gated items consume: R10, R11, R12, R13, R14's acceptance, R21, R22 and both R41 screens.
4. How the standing rule "re-pin, never edit" (rulings 2 and 10) applies to the acoustic baselines: `expectations.json` and the WAV-replay expectations are regenerated by running the harness, never hand-edited.
5. The rig accepts the manifests of the future R5 human corpus and R4 negative corpus, without building either.

**Kill criterion**

- If the joined rig cannot reproduce the harness's committed `expectations.json` baseline byte for byte on the 16 fixtures, the plumbing is wrong and no number from it may be quoted.
- Cheapest first experiment: run the existing harness and the existing rig on the same fixture and diff the transcript each believes it parsed.

**Out**

- Any decoder, DSP or grammar change (R11, R14, R15 and the rest are later items gated on this one).
- Building the R5 human corpus or the R4 negative corpus.
- The Phase B probes (a separate lab).
- Ranking two decoders on TTS-only audio (standing rule, critique C12).

**Gates**

- R10, R11, R12, R13, R14's acceptance, R21, R22, and both R41 screens.

---

This system is the joined rig: one run takes real audio through the real decoder on the native (Quest) pipeline, hands every decoder result to the real parser through a file, and scores the outcome at the command level a player feels. It sits between the push-audio harness (`NativeBridge~/harness/main.cpp`), which today keeps only the final `text` and passes on exact equality with `expectations.json` `expectedFinals` (`main.cpp:41-58`, `:227`), and the parser A/B rig (`Planning~/features/coverage-in-selection/ab-rig/`), which compiles the real parser from any git ref under dotnet in WSL (`stage.sh:15-25`) but reads text only (`Program.cs:304`) and has no scorer. It carries `Planning~/design-docs/automated-verification.md` §3 goals 1 and 2 — "Automated regression coverage for the acoustic pipeline (DSP → Vosk → parser) using real Vosk, runnable per change" (`:75`) and "Automated coverage of native-bridge logic that Claude can run directly from WSL, with no Unity and no device" (`:76`) — and extends Tier C's reach to the parser without reopening that design, whose backlog is complete (`:264`). It resolves the keystone fork in adr-0002, which layer is scored, and one constraint governs everything below: the kill criterion — a joined run whose pinned decoder configuration does not reproduce the committed `expectations.json` final texts byte for byte reports no command-level numbers.

> **What's decided.** The harness dumps every decoder result as JSON lines and the dotnet rig reads and scores the dump — two processes, one file contract (adr-0001); every run first checks that its pinned decoder configuration reproduces the committed `expectations.json` final texts byte for byte, and a decoder-side B's transcripts are listed, not checked (rule 2, adr-0005); phase 1 scores the parser layer and phase 2, a separate feature, the recogniser layer (adr-0002); each utterance lands in one outcome class, with slot partial credit, and wrong-intent fires and false fires are never netted against hits (adr-0003); the rig is an on-demand instrument and the harness stays the standing Tier C check (adr-0004); sealed cases are refused unless `--evaluate` is given, one side varies per A/B run, and recogniser-layer cases are marked, not missed (adr-0005). All numbers on this page are **provisional → the tuning pass** (the design intent is the load-bearing part).

## 1. The model

**The facts it rests on** (code-mapper recon, 2026-10-13, recorded in `Planning~/design-docs/joined-rig/recon-2026-10-13.md`).

- The harness replays 48 kHz WAVs through the native pipeline — ring buffer → downsampler → AGC → int16 → VOSK (`NativeBridge~/src/vosk_bridge.cpp:91-126`) — keeps only the final `text` (`main.cpp:41-58`), and passes on exact equality with `expectations.json` `expectedFinals` (`:227`).
- Quest and Android run that native pipeline. The Windows Editor runs its own managed C# pipeline, whose AGC has diverged from the native one (`Documentation~/native-bridge.md:61`; `01-code-audit.md:497`).
- The parser rig compiles the real parser source from any git ref under dotnet in WSL (`stage.sh:15-25`); it reads text only, with no words or confidences passed (`Program.cs:304`); it has no scorer; its grammar is a hand copy of the demo grammar, checked against the harness grammar pin by `--grammar` (`Program.cs:6-9`).
- The only intent/slot check is `Runtime/Testing/VoxrBatchTestRunner.cs`: intent and slots must match exactly, and the first failure ends the check (`:208`, `:303-329`). The research's citation `:230-233` is a comment, not the comparison.
- The recogniser decides what a player gets: `Runtime/Commands/VoxrCommandRecogniser.cs` is a 2,072-line `MonoBehaviour` driven by `Time.time`, and it owns the buffer window (split-utterance merging), eager flush, pending follow-ups and cooldown.
- The fixtures are 16 cases (`Tests~/Fixtures/audio/manifest.json`): clean 8, homophone 2, slot-variant 3, filler 1, split 1, silence 1. The split case expects one command across two finals.

**The rules** (design intent, not yet built).

| # | Rule | Settled | ADR |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Pipe | A file handoff. The harness gains a dump of every decoder result as JSON lines — finals and partials, words with `conf` and timings, and audio-time stamps — and the dotnet rig gains a reader and a scorer that consume the dump. Two processes, joined by one file contract; a dump is replayable through any parser ref without decoding again. Feasibility was shown on 2026-10-13 by lab `lab-next-level-probes` probe 1 (`Planning~/design-docs/joined-rig/recon-2026-10-13.md`): a few-line dump added to a scratch copy of the harness still matched 16/16. | adr-0001 |
| 2 | The run's built-in kill check | Every joined run first checks that its pinned decoder configuration reproduces the committed `expectations.json` final texts byte for byte. In a decoder-side A/B that is side A; in a parser-side A/B it is the only decoder side. If it does not match, the run reports no command-level numbers. A decoder-side B's transcripts are the change being measured, so they are not checked against the pin: they are listed per ruling 10, each case with its old and new transcript, and they become the new pin only through the harness re-pin at merge, never by hand. | adr-0005 |
| 3 | The layer scored (the keystone) | Phased. Phase 1 is the parser layer: each final goes to `Parse(text, words)`, and its outcome is scored. Phase 2 is the recogniser layer: the dump already carries partials and timestamps, so a later consumer can drive the staged recogniser on audio time. Phase 2 is a separate feature that follows phase 1. | adr-0002 |
| 4 | Scorer | Outcome classes plus slot credit (below). Wrong-intent fires and false fires are always reported separately and are never netted against hits (ruling 3). Any weighting of classes into a single number is provisional → the tuning pass. | adr-0003 |
| 5 | Role | An on-demand instrument, run for A/B measurement by the items it gates; their PRs list every flip, per the re-pin rule. The existing harness stays the standing Tier C check, and the verification binding is unchanged. | adr-0004 |
| 6 | Pipeline measured | The native (Quest) pipeline. The C# Editor pipeline and its AGC divergence are out of scope. | adr-0001 |
| 7 | Where it runs | Runnable by Claude from its own shell, with no Unity and no device. Which host runs each half (Windows-native or WSL) is decided in the architecture doc. This carries the intent of `automated-verification.md` goal 2; that goal's literal 'from WSL' named the host of its day and is not adopted here. | adr-0006 |
| 8 | Grammar | The demo grammar is the corpus grammar. The R5 pilot reuses the 16 demo phrases; the existing grammar-pin check (`Program.cs:6-9`) remains the sync guard; per-corpus grammars wait until a corpus needs one. | — |
| 9 | The sealed holdout | Refused unless `--evaluate` is given. Corpus manifests mark cases as sealed, and the rig skips them by default; it scores them only under an explicit evaluation flag, and stamps that report "holdout evaluation". Leakage becomes a deliberate act, never an accident. | adr-0005 |
| 10 | One side varies per A/B run | An A/B run varies either the decoder side (model, front end, endpointer) or the parser, with the other side pinned, and the report states which side varied. Under ruling 10, R14 and R15 together count as one decoder-side unit. | adr-0005 |
| 11 | Recogniser-layer cases are marked, not missed | Manifest cases that only the recogniser can resolve, such as the split fixture, are tagged. Phase 1 reports them as "not scored at this layer", never as misses, until phase 2 scores them. | adr-0005 |

**The outcome classes** (rule 4, adr-0003). Each utterance lands in one class:

- correct fire;
- right intent with wrong slot(s);
- wrong intent;
- missed (no fire);
- false fire on a negative.

Slot partial credit is the fraction of expected slots that match exactly; any formula beyond that is provisional → the tuning pass.

**One joined run, resolved.**

1. The harness replays the corpus and writes the dump (rule 1).
2. The kill check compares the final texts of the pinned side's dump (side A in a decoder-side A/B, the only decoder side in a parser-side A/B) with the committed `expectations.json` byte for byte; on any difference the run stops with no command-level numbers. A decoder-side B's transcripts are listed, not checked (rule 2).
3. Sealed cases are skipped unless `--evaluate` is given (rule 9); recogniser-layer cases are set aside as "not scored at this layer" (rule 11).
4. Each remaining final goes to `Parse(text, words)` on the parser ref under test, and its outcome lands in one class with its slot credit (rules 3 and 4).
5. The report names its corpus and the corpus's provenance and the side that varied (rule 10), keeps wrong-intent fires and false fires as their own counts (rule 4), and lists every flipped case with its old and new transcript and outcome (ruling 10).

## 2. The decisions it puts to its operator

The operator is whoever runs an A/B for a gated item.

1. **Which side to vary** (rule 10, adr-0005). The decoder side (model, front end, endpointer) or the parser, with the other side pinned; R14 and R15 together count as one decoder-side unit. Measuring both sides at once is never an option.
2. **Which corpus to run.** TTS fixtures are cheap and always available, but cannot rank decoders (critique C12). The human pilot or campaign is representative but scarce. The R4 negative corpus is the only source of a false-fire rate. No corpus dominates, because each answers a question the others cannot.
3. **When to spend the holdout** (rule 9, adr-0005). An evaluation run uses up its freshness, so it is reserved for a decision's final check.
4. **Whether a flip is acceptable.** Each pass→fail flip needs a named explanation (ruling 10).

## 3. Dynamics & failure modes

**Forward.** A joined run either refuses to report, because its pinned decoder configuration is off its pin (rule 2), or reports per-case outcome classes for one named corpus with one side varied (rule 10), with sealed cases skipped unless `--evaluate` is given (rule 9) and recogniser-layer cases reported as not scored at this layer (rule 11).

**Attacks on the rules, and what kills each.**

| Failure mode | What it looks like | Killed by |
|---|---|---|
| Overfitting to 16 TTS fixtures | A change tuned until the fixtures pass, which then fails on players. | C12: no TTS-only comparison ranks decoders. Every report names its corpus and the corpus's provenance. The sealed half of the human corpus (rule 9, adr-0005). |
| Hiding phantoms behind hits | A change that adds 2 hits and 1 false fire looks like +1 under one score. | Rule 4: the classes are never netted (adr-0003). |
| Misattribution | A parser change and a front-end change in one run move one number for two reasons. | Rule 10 (adr-0005). |
| Hand-edited baselines drifting from reality | A baseline edited by hand to make a run pass, so the pin no longer matches what the decoder emits. | Re-pin, never edit (rulings 2 and 10). The kill check (rule 2), which refuses numbers when the pinned decoder configuration is off its pin. |
| Small-N noise | One fixture flip is a sixth of a category. | Reporting counts per case and listing every flipped case with its old and new transcript and outcome (ruling 10). Any pass→fail flip needs a named explanation before merge. |
| The structural miss at phase 1 | The split fixture always looks like a miss at the parser layer and would sit in every baseline as noise. | Rule 11 (adr-0005). |
| Holdout leakage | A sealed case consulted while tuning, so the final evaluation no longer measures unseen speech. | Rule 9 (adr-0005). |

## 4. Boundaries & couplings

- **Owns:** the dump contract between the harness and the rig; the rig's dump reader; the scorer and its outcome classes; the joined report; the kill check; the sealed and recogniser-layer manifest marks and their handling.
- **Couples to:** the Tier C harness (locked `Planning~/design-docs/automated-verification.md`) — the dump is additive, and the harness verdict is unchanged; the parser A/B rig's staging (`stage.sh`); the demo grammar pin.
- **Hands off:** building the R5 human corpus and the R4 negative corpus — the rig accepts their manifests; phase 2 recogniser staging, to its own feature; unifying the four hand-synced grammar copies; the C#/C++ AGC divergence.
- **Does not decide:** any threshold or class weight; which decoder, front end or endpointer wins; any change to the decoder, the DSP or the grammar.

## 5. What it delivers

Every decoder-, grammar- and DSP-side item becomes measurable at the command level that players feel, by Claude from its own shell (`automated-verification.md` §3 goals 1 and 2):

- R10, R11 (phase 2), R12, R13, R14 with R15, R21, R22, and the R41 screens.
- Phantoms are kept visible as their own number.
- Each change is attributable to one side.
- The holdout stays sealed.

When it goes right, a PR for R14 with R15 shows exactly which fixtures changed outcome and why, and shows a separate false-fire count. Because the pinned decoder configuration still matches its pin, nobody has to argue whether the numbers can be trusted.
